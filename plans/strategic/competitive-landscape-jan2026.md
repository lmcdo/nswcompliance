## Insurance & Liability: The Hidden Battleground

### AI Solutions Panel Requirements

[Source: AI Solutions Panel Operating Guidelines PDF](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-04/ai-solutions-panel-operating-guideline.pdf)

Panel vendors must carry:
- **Public liability insurance**: $20M per occurrence
- **Product liability insurance**: $20M per occurrence and aggregate
- **Professional indemnity insurance**: $20M per occurrence

### The Professional Indemnity Problem

[Source: Kennedys Law](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2025/silent-ai-cover-the-unforeseen-risks-for-insurers/)

**"Silent AI" coverage gaps:**
- Many professional indemnity policies don't explicitly cover AI-related errors
- If AI causes a loss, insurers may argue it's outside coverage
- "If an engineer or architect relies on an AI tool that gives incorrect analysis or recommendations, the liability may fall outside the scope of traditional professional indemnity coverage"

**Key factor in liability:**
- "Level of human oversight exercised over AI tools"
- Professionals expected to "critically assess" AI outputs
- Leading AI companies (OpenAI, etc.) include limitation clauses shifting responsibility to users

### Your Competitive Angle

**Competitors use generative AI = potential liability exposure:**
- Archistar explicitly says outputs need human evaluation for "accuracy"
- DAISY uses Microsoft AI Foundry generative AI
- If AI hallucinates a provision that doesn't exist → professional negligence?

**Compliance API architecture = reduced liability:**
- No generative AI in production
- Every response cites verified provision from DB
- Audit trail proves exactly what was shown
- Provenance chain: response → provision → document_id → pdf_page → legislation_url

**Marketing message:**
> "Unlike AI tools that require human verification of every output, PlotDetect's responses are database lookups with guaranteed provenance. Your professional indemnity insurer can verify exactly where every claim came from."

## Other AI Reliability Developments to Track

### 1. NSW Agentic AI Guidelines (October 2025)

[Source: Digital NSW](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/generative-ai-basic-guidance)

New guidance for "agentic AI" - AI that makes decisions and plans actions:
- Requires "human intervention" definition
- Regular reviews of output
- Privacy and security controls

**Relevance:** As AI moves from advisory to semi-autonomous (e.g., auto-drafting DA responses), oversight requirements increase. Your audit trail becomes MORE valuable, not less.

### 2. Australian AI Consumer Law Review (October 2025)

[Source: Treasury Review](https://treasury.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-10/p2025-702329-fr.pdf)

Key findings:
- Prohibition on misleading conduct applies regardless of AI hallucination
- "That AI may 'hallucinate' does not warrant a re-examination" of consumer law
- Businesses must ensure AI technologies are "fit-for-purpose"
- Manufacturers liable for "safety defects" including AI-enabled products

**Relevance:** Generative AI that hallucinates provisions = potential misleading conduct. Your DB-lookup architecture = fit-for-purpose.

### 3. EU AI Act (August 2025 - August 2027)

[Source: EU AI Act Implementation](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/)

Timeline:
- February 2025: Prohibitions + AI literacy obligations
- August 2025: Governance rules + GPAI model obligations
- August 2026: High-risk AI system comprehensive compliance
- August 2027: High-risk AI in regulated products

**Relevance:** EU AI Act creates global precedent. Australian government watching. If EU requires audit trails for high-risk AI, Australia likely to follow.

### 4. OAIC AI Privacy Guidance

[Source: IAPP Global AI Governance](https://iapp.org/resources/article/global-ai-governance-australia)

Australian Privacy Commissioner position:
- AI hallucinations that infer personal details = collection of personal information
- Obligations around accuracy, security, deletion of data

**Relevance:** If AI infers incorrect information about a property owner, that's a privacy issue. Your verified DB = no inference, no privacy risk.
