# Try to break this

**PlotDetect — a one-page brief for students testing it**

---

## What it does

You type an address. It returns the planning controls that apply to that site — zone, height
limit, floor space ratio, minimum lot size, setbacks, heritage, flood and bushfire — and cites
each one to the clause it came from.

It is the desktop-analysis step you would otherwise do by opening the LEP and the DCP and reading.

**Start here:** the link your lecturer gave you. It has a code in it that tells us your feedback
came from this class — please use that link rather than typing the address of the site yourself,
or your reports land in a general pile.

No account. No password. Nothing to install.

---

## What I actually want from you

**I want to know where it is wrong.** That is the entire point of giving it to you.

I do not need to be told it is useful. I need to be told that the rear setback for your site is
missing, or that the number it gave you is not the number in the DCP you just read.

Please try it on **your own site** — the one you are already analysing for your assignment. A site
you know well is worth more to me than ten sites picked at random, because you can tell when the
answer is wrong.

### The three reports worth the most

**1. "This control is missing."**
You know from the DCP that a control applies. The tool does not show it.
Tell me: the address, which control, and where you found it.

**2. "This number is wrong."**
The tool says one thing, the DCP says another.
Tell me: the address, what it said, what the DCP says, and the clause.
**This is the single most valuable thing you can send me.**

**3. "I could not tell what this meant."**
A label, a number or a screen that made no sense, or that you read one way and it meant another.
Not a small point — if a planning student cannot read it, nobody can.

### Also useful

- A citation that does not go anywhere when you follow it.
- Something you expected to be there and was not.
- Anything you tried to do that the tool would not let you do.
- **A use I have not thought of.** If you find yourself wanting it for something it was not built
  for, that is worth more to me than a bug.

### Not useful

- "It looks good." I cannot do anything with that.
- Typos and colours — noted, but they are not why you are here.

## Things I already know are wrong

Told to you up front so you do not waste time reporting them, and so you know I am not pretending.

- **About 1 in 13 citations does not resolve to a clause.** Some point at a whole instrument —
  "LEP", "Apartment Design Guide" — with no clause number. They now say so, but they still do not
  take you anywhere.
- **Wingecarribee** controls are read from the Bowral town plan and served across the whole shire.
  The numbers are the same in the Mittagong and Moss Vale plans — that has been checked — but the
  table numbers differ, so a citation may not match the plan you open.
- **Coverage is uneven.** 28 councils have numeric controls. Outside those you will get much less,
  and that is not a bug.
- **Nothing here has been checked against a council's own determination.** It reads the published
  documents. It does not know what a council would actually approve.

**None of it is advice, and none of it is a substitute for reading the instrument.** If you use a
number in assessable work, check it against the source and cite the source, not this.

---

## How to tell me

The feedback button is on the page, bottom right. It already knows which address you were looking
at, so you do not have to repeat it.

Pick **Data Accuracy** or **Missing Information** for the two valuable kinds above. Leave your
email if you want an answer — you do not have to.

It takes about thirty seconds. Three good reports from you are worth more than a survey.

---

## What happens to what you send

I read all of it. Where you have found a real defect I fix it, and it goes through the same checks
as everything else — which means it gets a test that fails if the bug comes back.

If you have found something I did not know, I will tell you what happened to it.

Your feedback is tagged to this class, not to you. I do not collect your name, and the email box
is optional.
