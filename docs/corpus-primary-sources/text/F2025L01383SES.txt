                           SUPPLEMENTARY EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

        Issued by the authority of the Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards
                                                     Commission

                              National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013

         National Disability Insurance Scheme (Approved Quality Auditors) Rules 2025

Purpose of supplementary explanatory statement

This supplementary explanatory statement provides additional information on the
consultation undertaken in relation to the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Approved
Quality Auditors) Rules 2025 (the Instrument).

Additional notes on consultation

Extensive consultation informed the development of the Instrument.

Public consultation on the rule-making powers relating to approved quality auditors (AQA)
was undertaken through the exposure draft of the National Disability Insurance Scheme
Amendment (Getting the NDIS Back on Track No. 1) Bill 2024. This consultation
demonstrated strong support for the introduction of the rule-making power, including the
condition on approval in subsection 73U(5A) of the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act
2013 (Act) which requires AQAs not to employ or engage, or continue to employ or engage,
as members of an audit team or as key personnel, any person against whom a banning
order has been made. This requirement is reflected in section 10 of the Instrument.

The NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission (Commission) undertook consultations with
AQAs in March 2025, involving representatives of AQAs and the Joint Accreditation System
of Australia and New Zealand (JASANZ). A follow-up survey was conducted in May 2025 to
inform development of the Instrument, and further consultation on the draft Instrument
occurred in September 2025.

Feedback from JASANZ and AQAs showed strong support for the Instrument and identified
the need to strengthen eligibility requirements for AQA applicants, improve transparency and
management of conflicts of interest, and enhance audit quality.

The Instrument also reflects feedback on broader issues with the NDIS audit scheme. In
May 2024, the Commission held a workshop through the NDIS Commission Consultative
Committee focused on strengthening the participant voice in audits. Participants emphasised
the need to improve AQAs' understanding of human rights and to ensure audits prioritise
participant outcomes, safety and experience.

On 19 September 2025, the Acting Commissioner consulted state and territory Disability
Ministers on an exposure draft of the Instrument in accordance with the 28-day consultation
requirement. All jurisdictions supported the proposed changes. One jurisdiction suggested
expanding the matters the Commissioner may have regard to when revoking an approval as
an AQA. This suggestion was adopted and is reflected in section 8 of the Instrument.

All feedback received through consultations and surveys was considered in finalising the
Instrument.

Sections 6 and 11 of the Instrument respond directly to consultation feedback by
strengthening approval criteria, including mandatory consideration of conflicts of interest,
proposed mitigation strategies, applicants' understanding of the NDIS, and capability to
conduct participant-centred audit practices. These matters may also be considered when
determining whether to revoke an AQA's approval, supporting consistent participant-centred
audit across the NDIS.
