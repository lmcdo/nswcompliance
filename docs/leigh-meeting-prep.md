## What the product does — how to describe it in the meeting

Enter any NSW address and receive a structured PDF in under 60 seconds, drawn from five separate government data sources.

**Spatial constraints — what InfoTrack can't tell you.**
The constraints that actually derail projects: biodiversity sensitivity (BDAR trigger), riparian land, wetlands, flood planning area with ARI values, landslide risk, bushfire, acid sulfate soils, foreshore building line, classified road frontage, and key site overlays. InfoTrack marks most of these as "unknown." A s10.7 doesn't disclose them at all. Each is color-coded — green, amber, red — so the risk reads at a glance.

**DCP setbacks — 13 Sydney LGAs.**
Numeric front, side, and rear setbacks for dwelling house and secondary dwelling, extracted directly from each council's DCP. Current coverage: Ashfield, Blacktown, Campbelltown, Canterbury-Bankstown, Cumberland, Hornsby, Ku-ring-gai, Liverpool, Marrickville, Northern Beaches, Penrith, Waverley, Woollahra. New councils added in days when needed.

**SEPP pathway.**
Whether the site qualifies for CDC or requires a full DA, which code applies (Housing 2021, Seniors SEPP, TOD overlay), and whether BASIX or ISEPP is triggered.

**Zone, heritage, nearby DA activity.**
Live LEP zone and permitted uses. LEP and State Heritage Register listings. All DAs lodged within 200 metres in the last 12 months.

**The output.** A clean structured PDF. Not a portal. Something that can be attached to a file note or put in front of a client. Reads in two minutes.

## The offer — and the issues that need to be resolved first

The broad structure is right: free access for Leigh, revenue share on clients he introduces, his LGA priorities shape what gets expanded. But several things need to be settled before you can make the offer concrete.

**Pricing is undefined — which makes the rev share meaningless.**
20% of an undefined number is not an offer. Before you can talk rev share, you need at least a working pricing model. The right questions to ask Leigh first: how frequently would his builders use this (per project? per week?), and what do they currently pay for site research tools? That shapes whether per-report or subscription pricing makes sense, and at what level.

**"Free access permanently" needs to be defined.**
This probably means Leigh personally can run unlimited reports to validate accuracy and demo the tool. It shouldn't mean his clients get free access indefinitely — that's unsustainable. Be clear: Leigh's own access is free, permanently. His clients get a trial period, then pay.

**20% of year 1 only creates a cliff.**
Under the current structure, Leigh's financial stake ends after 12 months while he's still associated with the product. Consider either a lower ongoing percentage (10% for the life of the client relationship) or a one-time referral fee per converted client. Ongoing percentage aligns incentives better — he stays engaged, helps retain clients, and the relationship compounds. One-time fee is simpler to explain. Decide which before the meeting.

**What happens after year 1 is unanswered.**
Clients continue paying at full rate. Leigh has no ongoing incentive. If you go with rev share, either extend it at a lower rate or cap it at a defined amount per client. Don't leave this implicit.

## Questions that must come before the offer

Don't make the offer until you have answers to these — they determine what the terms should actually be.

1. Which LGAs are his builders and certifiers currently working in?
2. How often does a typical client need a site check — is this once per project, or multiple times during feasibility?
3. What do they currently spend on site research (InfoTrack, planning portals, consultant time)?
4. Is he confident enough in the accuracy to introduce it to one client now, or does he need to test it on settled addresses first?

His answers to 1 and 2 determine pricing. His answer to 3 sets willingness to pay. His answer to 4 determines what the immediate next step is.

---

## What agreement looks like

Not a formal contract. A clear verbal understanding on:

- Leigh has permanent free access to run reports for his own use
- Clients he introduces get a defined trial period, then move to a paid tier
- He earns [X]% of subscription revenue for [defined period] per introduced client
- He commits to: testing on a handful of known addresses, one or two introductions when he's comfortable, and direct feedback when something is wrong
- His LGA priorities go to the top of the expansion list

The meeting succeeds if you leave with: the LGAs his clients need, a date for the first introduced client, and a number he's comfortable putting in front of them.
