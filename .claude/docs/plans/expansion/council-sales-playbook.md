> "Think about your average government employee with an inbox stuffed with emails from concerned citizens. They're not going to give your cold email the time of day."

**Key insight:** SeamlessDocs found **90% of demos are booked via phone**, not email. Government employees live on the phone and aren't overwhelmed by cold callers like private sector.

## The Foolproof Approach: 5-Step Council Engagement

### Step 1: Phone First, Email Second

**Script for calling:**
> "Hi, I'm calling for Simone Plummer's office. My name is [X] from PlotDetect. We've built a planning compliance tool specifically covering Inner West's DCPs - Marrickville, Leichhardt, and Ashfield - and I understand the council received an AI planning grant. I'd like 15 minutes to understand how that's progressing and whether our provision database could help. Who would be the best person to speak with?"

**Why this works:**
- References something they care about (the $156k AI grant)
- Shows you did homework (specific DCPs by name)
- Asks for help navigating (government employees are helpful)
- Low commitment ask (15 minutes, understanding, not selling)

### Step 2: Build Your Champion

From [Close.com](https://www.close.com/blog/how-to-sell-to-governments):
> "Start by calling the relevant user buyers—they understand the value of your product. Get them to be your champions."

**Target the Strategic Planners:**
- Seth Dias - Strategic Planner (identified via LinkedIn/ZoomInfo)
- Strategic planning team handles DCP interpretation daily

**Champion discovery questions:**
- "What's your current process for checking DCP provisions?"
- "How often do applicants submit with wrong/missing information?"
- "Are you using any of the AI Solutions Panel tools yet?"
- "What's working? What's frustrating?"

### Step 3: Use Social Proof (Your Neighbor)

From research:
> "Governments hate to be first to anything. Show them how their neighbor increased efficiency."

**Your angle:**
- "We already have complete DCP coverage for your three former councils"
- "47,818 provisions with full citation chains to PDF pages"
- If you get ANY other council using it: "Canterbury-Bankstown / Newcastle is seeing X"

### Step 4: Leverage the AI Grant Timing

Inner West has $156k allocated and must show progress by **June 30, 2025**.

**Your pitch:**
> "I understand you received funding under the Early Adopter Grant Program. The panel solutions focus on pre-lodgement chatbots and document checking. We offer something complementary - a verified provision database with full audit trails. This could be the 'ground truth' layer that makes their AI tools more accurate."

### Step 5: Get Multiple Stakeholders Early

From [Close.com](https://www.close.com/blog/how-to-sell-to-governments):
> "Get all different buyers—user, economic, and executive—on board to help push through procurement."

**Buyer map for Inner West:**
| Buyer Type | Who | What They Care About |
|------------|-----|---------------------|
| User Buyer | Strategic Planners, DA Officers | Speed, accuracy, fewer applicant chaseback |
| Economic Buyer | Simone Plummer (Director Planning) | Efficiency metrics, risk reduction |
| Executive Buyer | Peter Gainsford (GM) | Council reputation, liability |

## What to Prepare: Before First Meeting vs Later

### BEFORE First Meeting (Essential)

| Asset | Purpose | Status |
|-------|---------|--------|
| **Live demo of Inner West DCPs** | Show you have their specific provisions | ✅ You have this |
| **One-pager** (not slide deck) | Leave-behind they can forward | Create |
| **3 discovery questions** | Understand their pain, don't pitch | Prepare |
| **Reference to grant program** | Show you understand their context | ✅ Research done |

**The one-pager should include:**
1. What PlotDetect does (2 sentences)
2. Why Inner West specifically (you have their 3 DCPs)
3. Key differentiator: "Every response cites exact provision + PDF page"
4. Call to action: "15-minute demo"

### AFTER First Meeting (Build Based on What You Learn)

| Asset | When to Create | Why Wait |
|-------|---------------|----------|
| **Compliance Dashboard** | After you understand what metrics they want | They'll tell you what they need to report |
| **Technical Whitepaper** | After technical buyer (ICT) is engaged | User buyers don't care about architecture |
| **Insurance Documentation** | After liability questions arise | Don't lead with defense |
| **Audit Trail Demo** | After compliance/risk buyer is engaged | Premature for planning staff |
| **Custom Pricing** | After you understand their budget/timeline | Grant is $156k - price accordingly |

## First Meeting: Discovery, Not Demo

From [GTMnow](https://gtmnow.com/first-call-disco-or-demo/):
> "At the enterprise level you have no shot at selling if you don't start with deep, layered discovery."

### Meeting Structure (30 minutes)

**First 5 minutes: Context**
- "Thanks for taking the time. I know Inner West received AI planning funding and I wanted to understand how that's progressing before showing you anything."

**Next 15 minutes: Discovery Questions**
1. "What's your current process when a planner needs to check a DCP provision?"
2. "How often do DA applicants submit with missing or wrong information?"
3. "I understand you're trialing AI tools from the Solutions Panel - how's that going?"
4. "What would success look like for the AI planning initiative?"
5. "Who else should I be talking to about this?"

**Last 10 minutes: Tailored Mini-Demo**
- Show ONE thing relevant to what they said
- Example: If they mentioned setbacks → show Inner West setback provisions with citations
- End with: "Would it be useful if I showed this to [the person they mentioned]?"

### What NOT to Do

❌ Don't start with "let me show you our product"
❌ Don't show the full platform (overwhelming)
❌ Don't talk about architecture, AI, compliance (too early)
❌ Don't send a 20-slide deck in advance
❌ Don't pitch to one person in isolation

## Pricing Strategy for Councils

From [Close.com](https://www.close.com/blog/how-to-sell-to-governments):
> "If a government has a spending threshold (e.g., $5,000), price strategically just below it to make sign-off easier."

**NSW Council Thresholds:**
- Under $5,000: Manager approval, no quotes needed
- $5,000-$50,000: 2 quotes required
- $50,000-$250,000: 3 quotes + formal RFQ
- Over $250,000: Full tender

**Strategy for Inner West:**

| Option | Price | Approval Level | Notes |
|--------|-------|----------------|-------|
| **Pilot** | $4,900 | Manager sign-off | Get in the door fast |
| **Annual License** | $49,000 | Director approval, 2 quotes | Under $50k threshold |
| **Enterprise** | $99,000 | Formal RFQ | Harder, but grant is $156k |

**Pilot pitch:**
> "We could do a 3-month pilot for $4,900 - that's manager approval level - and if it's useful, we can discuss a proper license."
