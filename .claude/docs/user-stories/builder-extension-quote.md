# User Story: Builder Quoting for Rear Extension + Second Storey
## Heritage Area Extension — Ashfield R2 Zone

**Last Updated:** 2026-02-05 (tested against production API)

---

## The User

**Dave Mitchell, Small-Medium Builder**
- 15 years experience in residential extensions and renovations
- Runs 2-5 active projects at a time
- Focus: rear extensions, second storey additions, heritage area renovations
- Quotes on fixed-price contracts (variation risk is on him)
- Bills $150-250/hour when not on site (quoting, admin, compliance checks)

**Pain point:** Missing one DCP requirement in a quote = variation cost he absorbs. Heritage jobs are high-risk because materials and design controls can double costs.

---

## The Job

**Client:** Homeowner at an Ashfield property (R2 zone, heritage conservation area)

**Proposed works:**
- Rear extension: 40 sqm ground floor living area
- Second storey addition: 60 sqm (3 bedrooms)
- Total: 100 sqm new floor area

**Quote requirements:**
- Exact buildable envelope (setbacks determine max floor area)
- Height and solar access compliance (affects roof design, costs)
- Heritage materials requirements (brick matching = 2x cost vs standard)
- Heritage design constraints (subservient design = more complex framing)

**Contract type:** Fixed price ($320k estimated)

**Risk:** If Dave underquotes by missing one control, he absorbs the variation cost. On a $320k job, a 5% underquote = $16k loss.

---

## The Pain Point (Manual Process)

### Step 1: Confirm zoning and heritage status (10 minutes)
1. Log into NSW Planning Portal
2. Enter client's address
3. Check LEP zoning → R2 Low Density Residential ✓
4. Check Heritage Map → property inside Heritage Conservation Area ✓
5. Note LEP max height: 9m for R2 zone
6. Screenshot for records

**Common issues:**
- Planning Portal slow (especially on weekends when builders do quotes)
- Heritage boundary unclear (edge cases)
- Max height in LEP might differ from DCP height planes (which applies?)

---

### Step 2: Download Ashfield DCP (10 minutes)
1. Google search: "Ashfield DCP 2016"
2. Navigate to Inner West Council website
3. Find DCP page — multiple PDFs listed:
   - Part A: Introduction
   - Part B: Residential Design
   - Part C: Heritage Conservation
   - Part D: Parking and Access
   - Part F: Development Category Guidelines
   - Chapter A: Miscellaneous
4. Download Part B, Part C, Part F (not sure which has setbacks)
5. Download total size: 80+ MB (slow on site internet)

**Common issues:**
- DCP is fragmented across 6+ PDFs (no single consolidated document)
- Unclear which Part contains which controls (setbacks in Part B or Part F?)
- Council website links sometimes broken or outdated

---

### Step 3: Find rear setback requirements (20 minutes)

#### 3a. Navigate Part B: Residential Design
1. Open Part B PDF (50+ pages)
2. Check Table of Contents — no "setbacks" entry
3. Search PDF for "rear setback" → 12 results
4. Skim through results to find relevant provision
5. Find: "Rear setback for dwelling houses: minimum 6 meters"
6. Note: applies to ground floor and upper floors

#### 3b. Cross-check Part F: Development Category Guidelines
1. Open Part F PDF (another 40 pages)
2. Search for "R2" to find R2 zone-specific controls
3. Find section on "Low Density Residential"
4. Cross-check rear setback: confirms 6m
5. Note any exceptions (heritage items might have different setbacks)

**Common issues:**
- Rear setback varies by dwelling type (detached house, semi, terrace)
- Heritage properties might have different setbacks (not clearly stated)
- Upper floor setbacks sometimes differ from ground floor (easy to miss)

**Time so far: 40 minutes**

---

### Step 4: Find height and solar access controls (25 minutes)

#### 4a. Check LEP height limit
1. LEP states: 9m max height for R2 zone
2. Measure existing house + proposed second storey: 8.5m total ✓ (complies)

#### 4b. Check DCP solar access planes
1. Return to Part B: Residential Design
2. Find section on "Building Height and Solar Access"
3. Find diagram showing solar access plane:
   - 3m height at rear boundary
   - Sloping at 45 degrees toward north
4. **Critical finding:** Solar access plane will clip the second storey roof
5. Calculate: need to step the roof down by 1.2m on the north side

**Cost implication:**
- Standard gable roof: $12,000 for materials + labor
- Stepped roof (to comply with solar plane): $15,000
- **Variation cost if missed:** $3,000

#### 4c. Check overshadowing rules
1. Find provision on overshadowing adjoining properties
2. Rule: "No more than 20% additional overshadowing of neighbor's windows and POS"
3. Dave doesn't have shadow diagrams software → assumes compliance (risky)

**Common issues:**
- Solar access diagrams in PDF are 2D (hard to visualize on actual site)
- Diagrams reference "north boundary" but site is oriented NW (how to apply?)
- Overshadowing calculation requires software (most builders don't have)

**Time so far: 65 minutes (1 hour 5 minutes)**

---

### Step 5: Find heritage materials requirements (30 minutes)

#### 5a. Navigate Part C: Heritage Conservation
1. Open Part C PDF (50+ pages of heritage provisions)
2. Check Table of Contents: find "Materials" section
3. Navigate to Materials section (page 18)
4. Read through 6 pages of materials controls

#### 5b. Identify key requirements
1. Find provision: "Face brick additions must match existing brick in color, texture, and bond pattern"
2. Find provision: "Modern materials (fiber cement, metal cladding) not permitted on street-facing facades"
3. Find provision: "Roof materials must match existing (terracotta tiles)"

**Cost implication:**
- Standard face brick: $80/sqm ($8,000 for 100 sqm extension)
- Heritage brick matching: $150/sqm ($15,000 for 100 sqm extension)
- **Variation cost if missed:** $7,000

- Standard metal roof: $6,000
- Terracotta tile roof (heritage): $10,000
- **Variation cost if missed:** $4,000

**Total materials variation if Dave misses these:** $11,000

#### 5c. Determine if brick matching is exact or "compatible"
1. Re-read provision: "must match existing brick in color, texture, and bond pattern"
2. Interpretation: exact match required (not just compatible)
3. Dave will need to:
   - Photograph existing brick
   - Get brick matched by supplier (PGH Heritage or similar)
   - Order samples, get client approval
   - **Extra time:** 2 weeks lead time for heritage brick

**Common issues:**
- "Match" vs "compatible" language is ambiguous
- No photos or examples in DCP to show acceptable matching
- Brick suppliers might not have exact match → need to argue "compatible" with certifier → risky

**Time so far: 95 minutes (1 hour 35 minutes)**

---

### Step 6: Find heritage design constraints (20 minutes)

#### 6a. Find "Additions" section in Part C
1. Navigate to "Additions and Alterations" section (page 24)
2. Read through 5 pages of design controls

#### 6b. Identify key constraints
1. Find provision: "Additions must be subservient to the original building in scale and form"
2. Find provision: "Upper floor additions must be setback from the main facade to maintain prominence of original building"
3. Find provision: "Roof form must be compatible with existing roof pitch"

**Design implication:**
- Second storey cannot be full-width across the front of the house
- Must setback upper floor by 2-3m from front facade
- Must match existing hip roof pitch (30 degrees)

**Cost implication:**
- Simple box second storey: $180,000
- Setback design with matching roof: $210,000 (more complex framing, more flashing details)
- **Variation cost if missed:** $30,000

**Common issues:**
- "Subservient" is subjective (how much smaller? 10%? 30%?)
- "Setback from main facade" — no dimension given (2m? 4m?)
- Dave interprets conservatively (3m setback) to avoid rejection → reduces floor area → lower quote

**Time so far: 115 minutes (1 hour 55 minutes)**
