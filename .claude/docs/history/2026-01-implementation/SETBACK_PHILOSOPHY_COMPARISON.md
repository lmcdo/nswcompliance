# Setback Display Philosophy: Objective Analysis

## TL;DR

**Marrickville & Leichhardt approach is CORRECT for their DCP structure.**

Don't fight the source material - show what the DCPs actually say.

---

## The 3 DCP Philosophies (Authoritative Citations)

### Ashfield DCP 2016 - Chapter F: "Prescriptive Numeric Tables"

**Quote from Chapter E2:**
> "Building setbacks:
> - Front: 6.0m minimum
> - Side: 0.9m or half the building height (whichever is greater)
> - Rear: 1.2m minimum to principal building"

**Philosophy:** Bright-line rules. Certifiers can tick boxes.

---

### Leichhardt DCP 2013 - Part C Section 2: "Contextual/Character-Based"

**Quote from Part C (West Leichhardt Neighbourhood):**
> "The streetscape is varied in terms of architectural styles as well as **setbacks and dwelling heights**"

**Quote from Young Street Distinctive Neighbourhood (C2.2.3.3):**
> "**Allow for contemporary development, which is complementary to the existing streetscape**"

**Philosophy:** Context matters. Setbacks must "complement existing streetscape" - no fixed number because every street is different.

---

### Marrickville DCP 2011 - Part 9 (Neighbourhoods): "Character Ranges"

**Quote from Precinct 13 (Henson Park):**
> "Front setbacks are generally consistent within each street with **a setback of 2 metres to 4 metres the most common**"

**Philosophy:** Describe the pattern. Designers match their neighbors, not a table.

## Pros vs Cons from User's POV

### Ashfield Numeric Approach

**PROS:**
- ✅ Clear pass/fail criteria
- ✅ Certifier can approve without discretion
- ✅ DIY homeowner knows exact number
- ✅ Fast DA process

**CONS:**
- ❌ Can produce cookie-cutter outcomes
- ❌ Doesn't adapt to site conditions
- ❌ May not suit heritage/unusual sites

---

### Marrickville/Leichhardt Contextual Approach

**PROS:**
- ✅ Produces better design outcomes (fits context)
- ✅ Allows flexibility for unusual sites
- ✅ Preserves neighbourhood character
- ✅ Professional designers prefer this

**CONS:**
- ❌ Requires design judgment (architect needed)
- ❌ Higher DA rejection risk if design poor
- ❌ Slower approval (more subjective)
- ❌ DIY homeowners feel lost

---

## Industry Best Practice: What Other LGAs Do

**Newcastle DCP 2023 (NEW - commenced March 2024):**
- Performance-based controls
- Provides numeric minimums BUT allows alternatives with good design justification
- **Hybrid approach** = Ashfield numbers + Leichhardt flexibility

**City of Sydney DCP 2012:**
- Mix of both:
  - Residential areas: Prescriptive setbacks
  - Heritage areas: Contextual controls
- Uses **interactive maps** to show existing streetscape patterns

**Industry Trend:**
→ Moving toward **performance-based with numeric guidance**
→ Newcastle's 2023 DCP is the gold standard

## Recommendation

**Accept the philosophical difference. Display it honestly.**

**For Ashfield:**
- Show numeric table
- Label: "Minimum requirements"

**For Marrickville/Leichhardt:**
- Show contextual text
- Label: "Character-based guidance (match existing pattern)"
- Add designer tip: "Measure 3 neighbors, use median"

**Users will understand** that different councils have different approaches.

**This is MORE professional** than pretending Marrickville has fixed minimums when it doesn't.

## What Newcastle Does (2024 Best Practice)

Newcastle DCP 2023 shows BOTH:

1. **Numeric starting point:** "Typical front setback: 6m"
2. **Performance criteria:** "May vary if demonstrates:
   - Consistency with streetscape
   - Adequate solar access
   - Privacy protection"

**We can do this for Marrickville/Leichhardt:**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ SETBACKS (Marrickville - Henson Park)   │
├─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Typical Pattern: 2m-4m (most common)    │ ← Show the range
│                                         │
│ Performance Criteria:                   │
│ ✓ Match adjacent properties             │
│ ✓ Maintain streetscape character        │
│ ✓ Preserve sight lines                  │
│                                         │
│ 📄 View DCP Part 9.13                   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

This gives users **practical guidance** without misrepresenting the DCP.
