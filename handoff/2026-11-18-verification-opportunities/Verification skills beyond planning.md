# Sell your verification rigour, starting with guardrails

**Recommendation: run three bets in parallel, all built on one clean, Apache-2.0 guardrails repo that must be published first.** The bets are (1) a fixed-price "agent guardrails install" as the fast-money offer, (2) a public "consultant-deliverable verification" demo built on Deloitte's A$97,587.11 refund as the positioning play, and (3) a "said done, wasn't" field study of your own hook interventions, which could grow into an evaluation of coding-agent overclaiming. Run two background channels alongside them: eval-labelled job applications (about A$140–170k base) and contract-recruiter registration (about A$900–1,100/day). The ranked ideas come from Claude Opus 5.5 (the intended Fable 5.2 was unavailable for lack of usage credits), and I checked each against the four research notes. The evidence quality needs stating plainly. The notes are mostly search-snippet level because official pages were blocked, and many success figures are vendor self-reports. The notes establish that the problem is real, costly and now a funded product category. They do not show that any small buyer will pay a solo developer for it. Every price in the shortlist (A$3–6k installs, A$4–6k/month retainers) is the idea generator's own assumption, tagged [unverified-own-knowledge]. That is why the plan below is mostly cheap tests with explicit stop conditions. One gate precedes everything: the existing repo cannot be published as-is (its LICENSE names Cole Medin, 25 tracked files / 91 MB of database dumps sit under `backups/`, and the full git history has never been secret-scanned), so repo #1 must be built from a clean tree after a licence fix, a full-history scan and key rotation.

The fast-money pick is the weakest on external evidence and the strongest on cost-to-test, so treat it as a hypothesis with a one-week kill test: classify the 76 contacts by proximity to a software team, then ask the warm replies one question about paying for a one-week install. The ideas file's own red-team says to stop if fewer than 10 of the 76 sit near a software team or none of the first 8 asked say "maybe", and to fall back to paid workshops. I agree with that stop rule. Where I depart from the ideas file is emphasis, not membership. B1 (ASRS evidence ledger, rank 7) has the best-dated, best-quantified Australian demand evidence of any idea, so it earns a parallel low-cost discovery test (eight messages, no build) even though it is not in the portfolio. The retainer idea (E3, rank 4) has no evidence of its own and should be treated as an add-on to B6, not a separate bet.

## The failure record is dollar-quantified, and verification is now a product

The anchor case is Australian. Deloitte's A$439,000 DEWR assurance review contained fake citations, phantom footnotes and a fabricated Federal Court quote ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/06/deloitte_ai_report_australia/)). The corrected report disclosed an Azure OpenAI GPT-4o tool chain that the original had not ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/deloitte-forced-to-refund-aussie-government-after-admitting-it-used-ai-to-produce-error-strewn-report)). DEWR asked for the final instalment back, **A$97,587.11 including GST** ([CFO Dive](https://www.cfodive.com/news/deloitte-refunds-60k-report-ai-errors-australian-government-accounting/803321/)), and the Greens called the refund "measly" and demanded all of it ([Greens](https://greens.org.au/news/media-release/greens-slam-deloittes-unethical-behaviour-and-measly-refund-over-ai-report)). A source-containment check ("does every quote and citation appear in its source?") is exactly what was missing, which is why this is the strongest opening line in the evidence. The caution is that the errors were reported as caught by a human academic, and the case shows the cost of absence, not that a buyer will purchase a checker.

Courts show the same pattern at higher volume. A Victorian KC apologised after fake quotes and citations in a murder trial, where the team checked the first citations and wrongly assumed the rest were correct ([eWeek/AP](https://www.eweek.com/news/australian-lawyer-apologizes-for-feeding-fake-cases-to-murder-trial-judge/)). NSW Supreme Court Practice Note SC Gen 23, in force since 3 February 2025, imposes citation-verification duties, and one summary says that verification cannot itself be done with a gen-AI program, which favours deterministic checkers ([Barry Nilsson via Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/australia/information-technology/1751692/nsw-supreme-courts-new-rules-for-ai-in-legal-practice)). That wording is from a snippet; the practice-note paragraphs were not read. A global tracker counted 1,598 hallucination cases by 9 June 2026, growing about 8 per day ([HAQQ](https://haqq.ai/blog/ai-legal-hallucination-audit.html)). Even products built to prevent this fail: Stanford found Lexis+ AI and Practical Law AI wrong more than 17% of the time and Westlaw AI-Assisted Research more than 34% ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-or-more-benchmarking-queries)), though that study is from 2024. Air Canada was held liable for its chatbot's bereavement-fare error, with damages of only C$812.02, which shows the liability principle more than the scale ([CRT decision](https://decisions.civilresolutionbc.ca/crt/crtd/en/525448/1/document.do)).

On the success side, quantified evidence is scarce and vendor-supplied. Abridge reports catching 97% of confabulations against 82% for GPT-4o on an internal benchmark of more than 10,000 encounters ([HLTH](https://hlth.com/insights/news/abridge-outlines-approach-to-eliminating-ai-hallucinations-in-clinical-notes-2025-08-25)). Melbourne's Heidi Health publishes a seven-layer evaluation framework and warns that a single hallucination-rate figure "says far less than it appears" ([Heidi Health](https://www.heidihealth.com/resources/evaluation-framework)). Neither is independently audited. The market signal is stronger than the outcome signal: Braintrust raised US$80M at an US$800M valuation in February 2026 ([FinSMEs](https://www.finsmes.com/2026/02/braintrust-raises-80m-in-series-b-funding.html)), and Qodo raised US$70M on the thesis that "verification will define the next phase of software development" ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/03/30/qodo-bets-on-code-verification-as-ai-coding-scales-raises-70m/)). Acquisition reports (Arize to Dynatrace, Galileo to Cisco) come from secondary sources and should not be quoted. Two inferences follow. Incumbents are folding evaluation into platforms, so people who design what to check gain value as tools commoditise. And the security-framed agent-verification field is crowded (Corridor, Symbiotic, Backslash), so the less-occupied niche is provenance and fabricated-value checking for agent outputs in data and extraction pipelines ([Corridor](https://fortune.com/press-releases/corridor-raises-25m-series-a-ai-coding-security-2026-03-18), [Straion](https://tech.eu/2026/02/24/from-prompt-and-pray-to-production-straion-raises-eur11m-to-govern-ai-coding-at-scale/)). That is an inference from the notes, not a measured gap.

## Where paid work is most likely: intermediaries and labelled roles, not direct enterprise sales

The notes' own conclusion is that solo-friendly entry points are boutique and mid-tier intermediaries rather than direct enterprise or agency contracts, and I agree. The best-quantified pocket is climate disclosure. Group 2 entities report for financial years starting on or after 1 July 2026; of 309 identified assurance providers, **238 have only Group 3 clients**, which signals a capacity problem ([AUASB](https://www.auasb.gov.au/media/ojfjxroz/preparedness-of-australian-auditors_-assurance-summary_web.pdf)). More than half of Group 1 entities used external expertise ([University of Sydney](https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2026/08/21/climate-reporting-drives-firms-to-build-capability-and-seek-expe.html)). That supports an "evidence ledger" (kWh, litres and GJ extracted from bills with page provenance and an auditor sampling pack), but no contract sizes or rates were found, and whether existing carbon-accounting platforms already ingest bills is unchecked [unverified-own-knowledge]. Government is the second pocket: the DTA's updated AI policy took effect 15 December 2025 with requirements phasing in through December 2026 ([DTA](https://www.dta.gov.au/articles/ai-policy-update-strengthening-responsible-use-across-government)), and NSW redesigned its AI Assessment Framework with registration and review for high-risk uses ([iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/nsw-gov-to-make-ai-risk-assessments-less-subjective-623185?edate=20260128&eid=1)). A BuyICT advertisement from Akkodis asks for "AI testing, evaluation and validation" with no published rate ([Akkodis](https://www.akkodis.com/en-au/careers/jobs/ai-engineer-canberra/6753ea86-4183-44c8-17c9-f1a02c3cdd04)). No tender for verifying AI-assisted consultant reports was found, so the Deloitte-style service is positioning first and revenue second.

For employment, eval work appears inside Applied AI titles rather than under an "AI evaluation engineer" label. Sydney examples are Airtree's "Founding Applied AI Engineer (Eval-Driven)" ([Airtree jobs](https://jobs.airtree.vc/companies/airtree-2/jobs/65473669-founding-applied-ai-engineer-eval-driven)), Culture Amp ([Blackbird](https://jobs.blackbird.vc/companies/culture-amp/jobs/71809669-ai-engineer-applied-ai)) and Checkbox's "Data Scientist, AI Evaluation" ([jobr.pro](https://jobr.pro/job/40659442/data-scientist-ai-evaluation)); the Canva evaluation-platform role is closed and the Airtree ad is from January, so all need re-checking. Pay evidence comes from one recruiter's data: A$140k mid and A$170k senior base, A$923 and A$1,120/day contract ([Galileo Search](https://galileosearch.com.au/ai-engineer-salary-australia)). The same background earns far less under a GIS label (A$64–96k, self-reported on [Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/research/AU/Job=Geographic_Information_Systems_(GIS)_Analyst/Salary/01cd4d9b/Sydney)), which is why relabelling is a day-2 action. Headwinds are real: Canva cut about 1,271 roles on 4 November 2026 ([HCA Mag](https://www.hcamag.com/ca/news/general/canva-job-cuts-affect-1271-roles-in-first-redundancy-round/596053)) and SEEK ads were down 3.5% year on year in September ([SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/australian-job-ads-edge-up-06-in-september)), so expect ex-Canva competition for A1. Mercor and Mindrift gig evaluation work is a cash bridge only; its rates were not captured, and the ideas file's A$60/hr floor is its own threshold.

## Volunteer and greenfield routes: visibility is documented, payment is not

Volunteer routes mostly buy credibility. UK AISI's Inspect Evals still welcomes fixes to existing evals, while new evals now go through a register ([inspect_evals README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_evals/main/README.md)). Paid maintenance contracts exist around the repo ([Manifund](https://manifund.org/projects/inspect-evals)), but no named contributor-to-contractor case was found. That makes scorer audits and the "mutation testing for eval scorers" idea (D4) good visibility per hour, with no direct payment route. The Black Spatula Project, an outsider-founded volunteer effort, reports flagging errors in scientific papers with roughly 10% false positives, and expert verification is its stated bottleneck ([CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/oopsie-prevention-ai-tools-now-scan-scientific-papers-to-catch-critical-research-errors)). A recomputation-plus-span-citation verifier addresses that directly, but the notes found no payment route. Hiring evidence for safety-adjacent programs is self-reported: Gray Swan says it hired staff and contractors from its arena ([Gray Swan](https://www.grayswan.ai/news/introducing-the-gray-swan-ai-proving-ground)), and Apart Research cites a METR hire ([EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/suvWKJbwfPDsQK3PM/how-apart-research-would-use-marginal-funding-to-scale-ai)). The notes conflict on whether Gray Swan has a 2026 challenge: one cites a US$300K relaunch on 20 October 2026 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/20/exclusive-ai-security-company-gray-swan-red-team-arena-contest-to-jailbreak-ai-agents/)), the other found none. Red-team competitions also reward adversarial creativity, not verification engineering, so they stay out of the plan.

For greenfield funding, most 2026 windows are closed. The open routes are rolling: Anthropic Fellows (US$3,850/week reported; remote-Australian eligibility unconfirmed) ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/anthropic-fellows-program)), Coefficient Giving's technical AI safety RFP (aggregator summary only) ([Granted AI](https://grantedai.com/grants/coefficient-giving-technical-ai-safety-research-rfp-alignment-control-cg-d0e1f2a3)), and Manifund micro-grants of US$5,000–50,000 ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/F8qBjWfAsryeTzbvY/regranting-in-2026-now-more-than-ever)). Coefficient Giving says it still "urgently need[s] more demanding tests of AI agents' capabilities" ([Coefficient Giving](https://coefficientgiving.org/?p=23764)). METR's own task bounty shows outsiders' work can land in a frontier eval (46 of HCAST's 189 tasks) and that a documented QA run was a payment condition, but it is paused ([METR](https://taskdev.metr.org/quality-assurance/)). Locally, Gradient Institute is AISI-AU's commissioned partner on agents interacting across organisational boundaries ([ai.gov.au](https://www.ai.gov.au/news-and-insights/blog/when-ai-agents-interact-new-research-australian-ai-safety-institute)), the nearest door for provenance work on agent messages, but no procurement panel or jobs were found. The D2 idea's raw material is real: the repo's own CLAUDE.md records PR #902 merging 10 seconds before its CI run was created and PR #907 sitting 40 minutes with no run. What is unknown is whether blocked claims were logged, which the day-1 count settles.

## Conclusion

The notes change the question from "where is the demand for verification?" to "who will pay a solo seller for it?" Demand for the problem is well evidenced: Australian dollar losses, court rules, funded vendors, mandated assurance. Willingness to pay by small buyers is not, and nearly every price in the shortlist is the idea generator's guess. The portfolio is therefore best read as a sequence of cheap experiments sharing one artifact, with a 2-week decision on which to continue.

The useful asymmetry is that the artifact costs the same whichever bet wins. A clean guardrails repo with measured catch rates feeds the install offer, the Deloitte-style demo, the field study, the eval-role applications and the recruiter conversations. If the day-1 and day-10 stop conditions fire, the fallback is paid workshops and contract roles, where the buyer purchases a deliverable or a day rate rather than a new product category.
