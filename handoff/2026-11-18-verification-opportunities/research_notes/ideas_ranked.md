**Sources.** Ideas draw on four evidence files in this folder: `case_studies.md` (CS), `volunteer_open_source.md` (VOS), `australian_demand.md` (AD) and `frontiers.md` (FR). Anything that rests on my own background knowledge is tagged **[unverified-own-knowledge]**. The evidence files are mostly built from search snippets, and their own caveats still apply.

**Bottom line.** The fastest money comes from selling the guardrail stack you already run, not from planning data. Offer it as a fixed-price "agent guardrails install" for teams adopting Claude Code or other agentic coding tools. Your positioning play is a public demonstration that a deterministic checker catches the kind of fabricated quote that cost Deloitte A$97.6k. Your greenfield bet is to turn your own hook-intervention history into an evaluation of AI coding agents' overclaiming ("said done, wasn't"). All three rest on one artifact: the clean, Apache-2.0 guardrails repo the handoff already recommends publishing first. Build that once and point everything at it.

### Ranked top 7

Ties at 18 were broken by two questions: does the idea reuse the shared artifact, and does it add a different kind of buyer?

1. **B6: Agent guardrails install (21).** This is the only idea where the product already exists and has been run in anger on your own codebase. The buyer is not domain-specific, so all 76 contacts are potential leads. The funded-startup evidence (FR §1) shows the problem is recognised. A fixed price removes the "hire an older contractor" friction because the buyer is purchasing a scoped deliverable, not a person. Lowest cost to test.
2. **A1: Applied AI Engineer, eval-driven (20).** Highest income ceiling. The job ads use your vocabulary: "measurable quality bars", "evaluation frameworks". Every other idea produces artifacts that strengthen this application, so it costs little extra to run alongside.
3. **B3: Consultant-deliverable verification (19).** The best positioning story in the evidence: a dollar-quantified Australian failure (A$97,587.11 refund) that your existing check addresses. Even if nobody buys the service, the demo is the strongest opening line for A1, B1, B2 and B7.
4. **E3: Fractional AI output assurance lead (19).** Scored on its own, but in practice it is the retainer that follows B6. It ranks here because recurring monthly income is what makes solo contracting viable, and because it turns "experienced" into the selling point.
5. **D2 (with D3 as step 1): Overclaiming eval from your own hook data (18).** The most original idea on the list. No one else has a year of labelled interventions against an AI coding agent on a real production codebase. D3 (the field study) is a cheap first step that pays off whatever happens next: a post, then a dataset, then a fellowship or grant application.
6. **A3: CWS/BuyICT contract route (18).** A low-effort background channel. Recruiters do the selling, and day rates are the highest reliable income in the evidence (A$900–1,100/day). It ranks below D2 only because it does not use your differentiating assets: recruiters match on titles.
7. **B1: ASRS evidence ledger (17).** This edges out A6, B7, D1 and D5 because it has the best-evidenced, time-bound Australian demand in the files: Group 2's first year is now, and assurers lack capacity. It also opens a buyer type (accounting and ESG) that is unaffected by the tech layoffs. B7 is its generic twin; if B1 discovery calls fail, reuse the pipeline as B7.

Just outside the top 7: **A6** (agent-verification startups: same artifact as B6, but remote AU hiring is unproven) and **D1** (the library underpinning B1, B2, B3 and B7; build it as a by-product rather than as a bet of its own).
