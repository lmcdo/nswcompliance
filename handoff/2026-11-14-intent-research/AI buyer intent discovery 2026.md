# Build the signal engine, sell the service

**Bottom line:** in late 2026, the state of the art for finding buyer intent online is a three-step pipeline. A cheap first pass collects candidate posts. An LLM then scores them and extracts the need. The top results go to an agent that researches them further. Nobody has shown that any tool reliably detects latent needs, meaning the needs of people who don't yet know what they need. Every commercial "AI intent scoring" product classifies needs people have already stated. The academic work that does infer unstated needs runs on interview transcripts or synthetic data, and about a quarter of its inferences are wrong.

For a solo NSW developer selling AI document processing, the decision follows from that. **Build the pipeline yourself, for a few dollars a day, and use it internally to win service clients. Don't sell it as SaaS.** Feed it mainly from public records and Australian sources, not Reddit or LinkedIn. Those two platforms are the most locked down and the most litigious, and Reddit's licensing terms are what forced GummySearch to close.

The market gap is real but narrow. Funded players (Clay at about a $7B valuation, Common Room now owned by Zoom) serve tech companies selling to other tech companies. The cheap Reddit-monitoring tools earn hundreds to a few thousand dollars a month and are exposed to platform risk. Nobody visibly serves non-tech Australian SMB verticals with signals from public records. That gap is a conclusion drawn from not finding anyone, not a confirmed opening.

The legal risk that matters to you in NSW isn't the CFAA, which is US law. It's the Australian Privacy Act, the statutory privacy tort in force since 10 June 2025, and the Spam Act's consent requirement. Those make per-person lead dossiers and cold email to scraped addresses the riskiest things you could build.

## Most "AI intent" tools are keyword filters with an LLM upsell

The commercial landscape has settled on one design, sold at prices that range from free to six figures. At the bottom, **F5Bot is free** and covers all public Reddit posts and comments plus Hacker News, alerting within minutes; it now sells paid tiers with "AI semantic alerts" and webhooks ([F5Bot](https://f5bot.com/)). Syften runs about **$30–120/month** with boolean filters, and AI filtering is reserved for higher tiers ([RedShip, vendor-authored](https://redship.io/best-reddit-monitoring-tools)). Octolens runs every mention through AI relevance scoring across Reddit, X, LinkedIn, HN, GitHub and more, from roughly **$159/month**, with real-time refresh only on the **$499 tier** ([peeklens](https://peeklens.ai/blog/octolens-review)). Enterprise suites like Brandwatch (estimated median contract around $50k/year) and Talkwalker focus their AI on sentiment for brand and PR teams, not purchase intent ([marketintelligencetools](https://marketintelligencetools.com/reports/social-listening-pricing/)). Treat every price here as approximate. Third-party sources conflict, and almost every comparison page is written by a vendor that ranks itself first.

The users' main complaint is noise, and that complaint is where the opening lies. G2 reviewers of Mention report "many irrelevant mentions" and "alert fatigue" out of the box ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/mention/)). One practitioner's tool flagged 47 mentions in a week, and only three needed action ([Cotera, vendor opinion](https://cotera.co/articles/social-listening-tools-ai)). Founders relayed by roundups describe keyword false positives as "brutal", mostly competitors promoting themselves ([superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/articles/best-reddit-social-listening-tools)). The competitive axis is precision with an attached reason, not coverage. A Claude triage layer on top of a free feed attacks exactly that problem, which is why the paid tiers exist. No independent precision/recall comparison of these tools exists.

The signal-based selling tier went through heavy consolidation in 2025–26. **Zoom announced its acquisition of Common Room on 2 July 2026 and completed it on 17 July**, for a reported $250–267M ([Zoom IR](https://investors.zoom.us/news-releases/news-release-details/zoom-acquire-common-room-bringing-buyer-intelligence-its-ai)). Trigify, which tracked LinkedIn engagement signals, reportedly shut on 22 October 2026 ([signado](https://signado.io/blog/trigify-alternatives)). Koala is reported dead ([moderninbound](https://moderninbound.com/blog/koala-vs-warmly)), and Warmly is reportedly going to HubSpot ([hackingdemand](https://hackingdemand.com/blog/warmly-alternatives-after-hubspot-acquisition)). UserGems costs roughly **$33–40k/year plus implementation** ([industry-lens](https://industry-lens.com/compare/warmly-vs-usergems)). Third-party account-level intent data (Bombora, 6sense, G2) is built on US enterprise software research behaviour. The only near-independent accuracy figures are second-hand: about 81% precision for Bombora and 87% for G2, from a "Brixon Group" test whose original couldn't be located ([autobound](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/top-15-intent-data-providers-compared-2026)). One reviewer's guess is that only 20–30% of "surging" accounts have genuine intent ([marketbetter](https://marketbetter.ai/blog/bombora-review-2026/)). None of this fits a seller targeting small Australian firms.

The realistic paid tools for a solo operator are these:

- **Apollo**: the free tier includes buying-intent topics ([autobound](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/top-15-intent-data-providers-compared-2026)).
- **Clay**: the free tier has 500 actions a month; Claygent research costs roughly **$0.15–1.12 per lead** ([Octave](https://www.octavehq.com/post/what-is-claygent-clay-ai-research-agent-guide)).
- **Credit-based research agents**: Origami, Explee and Persana, whose pricing is contested.

The churn rate in this tier is itself the main argument against building workflows on rented signal platforms.

The founder-oriented "pain mining" tier is losing its reference product. **GummySearch stops all service on 1 December 2026**, having closed to new users on 30 November 2025 ([okara](https://okara.ai/blog/gummysearch-alternatives)). It closed because it couldn't reach a commercial Reddit licence ([Failory](https://newsletter.failory.com/p/when-reddit-pulls-over)). Its successors include PainOnSocial (about $19/month) and BigIdeasDB, whose entire independent record is about four reviews ([preuve](https://preuve.ai/blog/bigideasdb-review)). They cluster complaints people have stated outright, and their "validated idea" scores have never been checked against outcomes. Several small open-source repos implement the same pipeline for free, and they make useful templates:

- **Pain-Point-Miner**: LLM extraction, then embedding clustering, then scores for intensity, urgency and monetisation ([GitHub](https://github.com/alexgutscher26/Pain-Point-Miner)).
- **reddit-complaint-miner**: a VADER sentiment pre-filter so the LLM only sees likely complaints ([GitHub](https://github.com/stelatoris/reddit-complaint-miner)).
- **pain-discovery**: local Ollama models across Reddit, HN and Trustpilot ([GitHub](https://github.com/albertorsesc/pain-discovery/blob/main/README.md)).

Licences, maintenance status, and whether their keyless Reddit access still works weren't checked.

## Fine-tuning closes the gap with human analysts; prompting alone does not

The most rigorous evidence on extracting needs from user-generated text comes from marketing science. Timoshenko, Mao and Hauser ran a blind comparison with a consulting firm. They found a **supervised fine-tuned LLM matched or beat professional analysts at extracting customer needs, while the base, prompt-only model was "not sufficiently accurate or specific"** ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5167493); [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/large-language-models-can-help-professionals-identify-customer-needs)). A secondary citation puts the training set at about 600 labelled examples ([arXiv 2608.16467](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16467)). That figure is snippet-level and unverified against the paper. The practical implication is that **your labelled gold set is the asset, not your prompt.** A few hundred posts hand-labelled as "a document-processing buyer, or not" is enough to fine-tune a small classifier, calibrate thresholds, and measure whether anything works.

Expected accuracy depends on how fine-grained the question is. Binary relevance tasks reach high numbers. Fine-tuned BERT flagged requirement-relevant app reviews at **F1 0.92** ([arXiv 2409.15473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15473v1)), and zero-shot ChatGPT hit **F1 0.84** with a tuned prompt ([arXiv 2505.04759](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04759v1)). Fine-grained multi-class typing with lightweight open models falls to **F1 0.32–0.74** ([arXiv 2510.23055](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23055v1)). These figures are snippet-level and haven't been checked against the full PDFs. "Is this a person with a specific, solvable document problem, at a firm that can pay?" is the fine-grained kind of question, so expect roughly 0.5–0.75 before fine-tuning. That estimate is inferred, not measured. **No peer-reviewed benchmark exists for detecting B2B buying intent in forum or social posts.** It is a genuine evidence gap, so whatever you build, you'll be measuring it yourself.

Detecting latent needs, meaning pain in someone who doesn't realise a solution exists, is where the evidence is thinnest:

- **LENS (2026 preprint)**: an LLM working on stakeholder interview transcripts reached F1 84.4% on *explicit* requirements. Experts judged about **75% of its inferred latent requirements useful**. Its false positives included inventing automation needs for tasks that were already automated ([arXiv 2606.25867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25867)). That is clean interview data, not noisy forum posts.
- **AURA**: reports 0.804 implicit-need coverage against 0.223 for a literal-only approach, but on a synthetic benchmark judged by gpt-4o-mini ([arXiv 2606.05557](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05557)).

Read together, latent inference works partially, needs human review, and stays unproven on social text. The workable approach is to **infer latent need from observable situation rather than from complaint language**. A post describing someone retyping invoices from PDFs every Friday never says "I need document AI". An LLM can extract the structured fields (task, volume, current workaround, tools named, role, deadline pressure) and a deterministic rule decides whether that pattern implies an automatable document workflow. This "explicit elements imply implicit intent" framing has a 2026 preprint arguing it makes inference auditable ([arXiv 2606.11207](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11207)).

Detecting each discussant's state (frustration, workaround language, sunk cost, time pressure, role, business size) has no validated marker set for buyer intent. Only churn-intent work from 2018 ([CoNLL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.08432)) and unvalidated rule-based frustration patents exist. Inferring the author's attributes works alarmingly well. Across nine LLMs on real Reddit profiles, models inferred location, income and similar attributes at **up to 85% top-1 and about 95% top-3 accuracy, 100× cheaper than humans** ([Staab et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07298)). That gives you strong qualification context (role, firm type, location) from posting history. It is also exactly the profiling the paper frames as a privacy violation, which is a live issue under Australian law. Use thread context freely. Pull author history only for shortlisted items, only for business-relevant attributes, and don't store the resulting profiles.

## Cascades cut costs, but not when false positives burn reputation

Cascades are well evidenced. FrugalGPT reported matching GPT-4 quality at **up to 98% lower cost** with a learned cascade ([arXiv 2305.05176](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.05176)). In 2025, semantic-agreement cascades, which sample several small models and escalate when they disagree, reached target-model quality at **about 40% of compute without a trained router** ([arXiv 2509.21837](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21837v3)). A 2026 agent study matched large-model performance while calling the large model on **about 15% of steps**, using perplexity-based deferral ([arXiv 2604.07036](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.07036)).

There are two caveats you should design around. First, an LLM's stated confidence ("I'm 90% sure") is systematically overconfident ([Xiong et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13063)). Escalate on disagreement between samples, or on token margin, never on the model's self-report. Second, an economic analysis found **a single large model beats a small-to-large cascade once the cost of an error is high enough** ([arXiv 2507.03834](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.03834)). For outreach, a false positive means contacting someone with an irrelevant pitch, which costs reputation in a small market like NSW. Use the cascade for recall and cost on the broad funnel. Spend frontier-model and human attention on the last stage before contact.

At current Claude pricing the economics are trivial at triage scale:

- **Haiku 5.5 costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens; Sonnet 5.5 costs $2/$10; Opus 5.5 costs $4/$20** ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)).
- The Batch API halves costs ([batch docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/batch-processing)).
- Prompt caching cuts repeated rubric input by about 90% ([caching docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)).

Triaging 10,000 items a day at about 1,000 tokens each comes to roughly **$1/day of input before discounts**; that figure is my arithmetic on the listed prices. For comparison, the founder of Leadverse, an indie Reddit-lead tool, says LLM calls are about 90% of its costs ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/launching-countless-apps-until-one-gained-momentum-and-hit-3-3-k-mrr-U9btJ44RYRcAaukCqHoj)). At Haiku 5.5 prices that constraint largely disappears. For grouping repeated pains across communities, BERTopic (embeddings, UMAP, HDBSCAN) remains the cheap baseline. TopicGPT matches human labels better (harmonic purity 0.74 vs 0.58), but costs more ([arXiv 2311.01449](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.01449)).

Agentic research has one production reference point. Clay reportedly runs **300M+ Claygent runs a month and had to rebuild its evaluation programme after early evals proved inadequate** ([ZenML, secondary](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/production-evaluation-and-data-foundations-for-go-to-market-agents)). The lesson for you is to budget for evaluation from day one.

Any agent that browses also has a security problem that is now measured in the wild. Researchers found **15,300 validated indirect prompt injections across 1.2 billion URLs, about 70% hidden in non-rendered HTML** such as comments, headers and metadata ([Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/researchers-10-wild-indirect/?from=kiberboloid); [arXiv 2604.27202](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.27202)). Strip non-rendered HTML before Claude sees scraped text. Never let fetched content trigger sending, CRM writes or tool escalation without validation. Require every lead fact to carry a verbatim quote and source URL, and reject any fact without one. That last rule is also your main defence against hallucinated lead details, for which no published error-rate measurement exists.

Timing evidence is mostly vendor-made. Vendors report trigger-based outreach replies of 4–8% (conservative) up to 15–40% (stacked signals), against 1–4% for cold outreach ([Puzzleinbox](https://puzzleinbox.com/blog/cold-email-trigger-events-job-change-funding-hiring-2026/); [OneAway](https://oneaway.io/blog/best-email-outreach-signals-triggers-in-2026-ranked)). Instantly's benchmark puts the average cold-email reply at **3.43%** ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)). One indie founder self-reports **35% replies to DMs sent to Reddit posters** ([Bazzly summary](https://onepage-research.sliplane.app/articles/indiehackers.com/bazzly-0-to-600-mrr-reddit)). The direction is consistent, the magnitudes are unreliable, and no controlled study exists. Run your own holdout group.

## Reddit, LinkedIn and Facebook Groups are the worst data bets

The platforms richest in pain-point talk are now the hardest and riskiest to read programmatically. The table ranks sources by legitimacy of access for a commercial user in NSW. It is not legal advice, and several rows rest on background knowledge that wasn't re-verified this session.

| Source | Legitimate route (late 2026) | Cost | Risk for commercial lead use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reddit | API approval required under the Responsible Builder Policy; free tier is non-commercial; commercial terms by contract only | Unpublished; ~$12k/yr minimum reported (unverified) | **High.** Reddit is suing indirect scrapers, including through Google SERPs |
| LinkedIn | Sales Navigator seat, ads, manual engagement; APIs gated behind partner programmes | Seat pricing not retrieved | **Highest.** Proxycurl was sued into shutdown in 2025 |
| Facebook Groups | No API (Groups API removed April 2024); join as a genuine member, partner with admins, run your own group, Lead Ads | Ads spend | **High** for any logged-in scraping |
| X | Pay-per-use API; or xAI's X Search tool | ~$0.005/post read; xAI $5 per 1k posts plus tokens | **Low** via first-party routes |
| Hacker News, Bluesky, YouTube comments, Stack Exchange | Official APIs and firehoses | Free or quota-based | **Low**, but low yield for AU SMBs |
| Discourse forums, Whirlpool, AU trade forums | Ask the operator; follow each site's terms | Free | **Low with permission**, otherwise unclear |
| Web discovery | Brave, Exa or Tavily search APIs, then read the source through its official API | ~$5–24 per 1k requests | **Low** for discovery; no cover for the scraping that follows |

**Reddit** closed self-service API registration in late 2025. Access now requires explicit approval under its Responsible Builder Policy ([xpoz](https://www.xpoz.ai/blog/guides/reddit-api-pricing-tiers-and-alternatives/); [Octolens](https://octolens.com/blog/reddit-api-pricing)). These are third-party summaries, because Reddit's help centre couldn't be fetched. The free 100-queries-per-minute tier is described as non-commercial ([Data365](https://data365.co/blog/reddit-api-pricing)). Unconfirmed vendor sources claim unauthenticated `.json` endpoints began returning 403 in May 2026 ([socialcrawl](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/blog/reddit-data-api-2026)). Most important: in **October 2025, Reddit sued SerpApi, Oxylabs, AWMProxy and Perplexity for harvesting Reddit content indirectly via Google results** ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/reddit-sues-perplexity-serpapi-scraping-google-463681)). So pulling Reddit through a SERP API is exactly the conduct being litigated, not a workaround. A single low-quality source reports the SerpApi dismissal motion was largely denied in July 2026 ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/reddit-lawsuit-against-serpapi-survives-dismissal-motion)).

Your legitimate options on Reddit are these:

- Apply for API access honestly.
- Read and participate manually.
- Use a vendor that can show a Reddit agreement in writing. No source confirmed which vendors hold one.
- Buy Reddit Ads against the same communities.

**LinkedIn** shows why "public data" arguments don't protect a small operator. hiQ won its CFAA rulings but lost on breach of contract in 2022, settled, and destroyed its data; this is background, not re-verified ([Ninth Circuit](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/04/18/17-16783.pdf)). LinkedIn sued Proxycurl in January 2025. Proxycurl, a business with roughly **US$10M in revenue**, shut on 4 July 2025 because legal fees would have been unrecoverable even if it won ([Nubela](https://nubela.co/blog/goodbye-proxycurl/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-wins-legal-case-data-scrapers-proxycurl/756101/)). Even a defensible position is unaffordable for a solo developer. Use Sales Navigator, ads and manual engagement.

**Facebook Groups** are probably where NSW tradespeople, property managers and small-practice owners actually talk. They have no developer route at all. The Graph API's Groups access was removed from 22 April 2024 ([Meta changelog](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/changelog/version19.0/)). CrowdTangle closed in August 2024. The Meta Content Library is limited to academic and non-profit researchers working inside a Meta-controlled environment ([Social Media Lab](https://socialmedialab.ca/web/?p=22554)). *Meta v. Bright Data* (January 2024) held only that Meta's terms don't bar a non-user's logged-out scraping of public pages. It is a non-binding district decision that Meta chose not to appeal, and it leaves logged-in scraping, where Group content lives, clearly prohibited ([Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/business-litigation/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/)). The routes that work are genuine membership under each group's rules, partnerships with group admins, your own group, and Meta Lead Ads, which collect consented contact details.

**X** has become a clean, priced route. Pay-per-use became the default on 6 February 2026, and Basic subscribers were auto-migrated from 1 June ([Open Terms Archive](https://opentermsarchive.org/en/memos/x-replaces-its-dollar200-entry-level-api-plan-with-a-pay-per-use-model/)). Reads cost about **$0.005 per post** ([postproxy](https://postproxy.dev/blog/x-api-pricing-2026)), so 100,000 posts a month is about $500. xAI's X Search tool bills **$5 per 1,000 posts and $10 per 1,000 profiles, plus tokens**, since 21 September 2026 ([xAI pricing](https://docs.x.ai/developers/pricing)). Cap the agent's tool calls, because each returned parent or quoted post also counts. On general web discovery, Google's Custom Search JSON API shuts on 1 January 2027 and Bing's APIs were retired in August 2025 ([Brave](https://brave.com/learn/google-api-shutdown/)). That pushes discovery to Brave (about $5 per 1,000 requests), Exa or Tavily ([beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/tavily-vs-exa-vs-brave-vs-perplexity-search-api-agent-cost-2026)). Using a scraping vendor doesn't launder your exposure under a platform's terms or under privacy law, and two such vendors are defendants in Reddit's suit.

### Australian law, not platform ToS, sets your real ceiling

For someone based in NSW, the decisive exposure is privacy and spam law:

- **Privacy Act.** Scraped names, handles and posts linked to people remain personal information. The OAIC has made determinations that scraping interfered with privacy ([DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/09/australia-scraping-the-barrel-when-data-scraping-breaches-the-privacy-act/)). Its AI guidance says building a dataset by scraping "may constitute a covert and therefore unfair means of collection" ([iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/oaic-draws-lines-on-data-usage-for-generative-ai-training-612500)).
- **Statutory privacy tort.** A tort for serious invasions of privacy has applied since **10 June 2025**, regardless of business size ([OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/more-privacy-rights/statutory-tort-for-serious-invasions-of-privacy)).
- **Small-business exemption.** It plausibly covers a solo developer under A$3M turnover, but it reportedly doesn't apply to anyone trading in personal information, such as selling leads. That point is background and not re-verified, so check it with the OAIC before you productise.
- **Spam Act.** Commercial email and SMS need consent. Inferred consent requires an ongoing relationship, and the sender bears the onus of proof ([Allens](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/explore/2025/food-and-beverage-law-bulletin-2025/emerging-regulator-views-and-enforcement-risks-in-direct-marketing-and-spam/)). Enforcement is real: Tabcorp paid **$4.0M** in June 2025 ([ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2025-06/tab-penalised-4-million-spamming-vip-customers)), and SpinTel received a $653,400 infringement notice published in November 2026 ([ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-11/Infringement%20Notice%20-%20SpinTel%20Pty%20Ltd%20-%20Spam%20Act%202003_Redacted.pdf)).

Put together, the risk depends on what you store and how you contact people. **Aggregated, de-identified pain themes are low risk. Per-person lead profiles assembled from posting history, then cold-emailed, stack every exposure at once.** The defensible operating pattern is this: detect at the level of a business or a public record, reply publicly within community rules, or contact a firm through its published business channels with a relevant, individually written message, and let the person opt in.

## Public records beat social posts for latent document pain

People with the most document-heavy pain often don't post online. They are older SME owners, trades businesses and small professional practices. Reddit's vocal, tech-savvy skew is acknowledged but unquantified ([echai.ventures](https://echai.ventures/how-founders-use-ai/market-research/how-do-i-mine-reddit-reviews-and-online-communities-with-ai-to-find-real-pain-points)). Their pain does leave traces in public events, and those traces are both legally cleaner and closer to real latent need than complaints. Three examples:

- A firm advertising on SEEK for data-entry or admin roles.
- A builder lodging a stream of development applications.
- A practice affected by a regulatory change that adds paperwork.

No tool packages "hiring for admin roles means budget for automation" as a feature. You'd build it yourself. The plumbing exists: TheirStack (about $49/month), Coresignal (from $49/month) and PredictLeads (100 free calls, then $0.04 per credit) sell job-post data ([revenueflow](https://www.revenueflow.com/blog/best-theirstack-alternatives); [decanaria](https://decanaria.com/providers/best-job-posting-data-providers)). Their Australian coverage wasn't established. **SEEK has no job-search API**, and the Apify actors that read its public JSON endpoint are unsanctioned ([jobspipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/seek-api); [Apify](https://apify.com/rel8ble/seek-jobs-scraper)), so check SEEK's terms before relying on them. Upwork killed RSS on 20 August 2024 ([Upwork Support](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/52052528243731-RSS-deprecation)). Its proposals see about **7% replies** in AI/ML, from vendor data on 133,872 proposals ([GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-market-report-2026)).

Demand-side data supports a done-for-you service over a self-serve tool:

- Australian SME AI adoption sits around **41–44%** per the National AI Centre tracker ([Minister's release](https://www.minister.industry.gov.au/ministers/timayres/media-releases/australian-ai-adoption-tracker-report-shows-business-harnessing-ai); [ai.gov.au](https://ai.gov.au/news-and-insights/blog/ai-adoption-insights-december-2025-february-2026)).
- **46%** of SMEs aren't using AI and don't plan to in the next year ([MYOB via IT Brief](https://itbrief.com.au/story/myob-says-ai-using-smes-are-growing-2-8-times-faster)).
- 23% say they lack time to learn it ([Xero via IT Brief](https://itbrief.com.au/story/xero-study-says-ai-unlikely-to-cut-australian-jobs)).
- Accounting practices using AI report saving **8–11 hours a week** ([Xero](https://www.xero.com/my/media-releases/australias-top-accounting-and-bookkeeping-practices-leveraging-ai-for-growth-not-job-cuts/)).
- NAB's headline finding is that adoption is highest among finance and property SMEs ([Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/digital-transformation/13502-ai-adoption-highest-for-finance-and-property-smes-says-nab)).

No Australian survey quantifying document processing specifically by vertical was found. How solo automation builders actually win first clients is consistently, though anecdotally, through **warm networks, referrals and visible demos**, not cold lists ([n8n community](https://community.n8n.io/t/looking-for-advice-how-did-you-find-your-first-n8n-client/200705); [dev.to](https://dev.to/ottoaria/how-to-find-your-first-ai-automation-client-in-2026-without-a-portfolio-54ld)). A sniffing system works best as a way to time and personalise that motion, not to replace it.

### Recommended architecture

The design below combines the cascade evidence, the injection findings and the legal constraints. It is a synthesis, not a published reference system.

| Stage | What it does | Tooling | Approx. cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Sources | Public records (NSW DA/planning data, tenders, ABN/ASIC events), job posts (TheirStack/PredictLeads), X via API or xAI, HN/Bluesky/YouTube via APIs, permitted forums, web discovery via Brave/Exa | Python collectors on cron | $50–150/mo data |
| 2. Recall | Embed items; semantic match against 20–40 written "pain prototypes" (e.g. "re-keys data from PDFs into accounting software"), plus a cheap keyword net | Local embeddings, pgvector | ~$0 |
| 3. Triage | Haiku 5.5 extracts JSON (task, document type, volume, workaround, role, firm type, AU location, verbatim quote); a deterministic rule scores need, fit and timing | Batch API + cached rubric | ~$1–3/day |
| 4. Escalate | Only when 3 samples disagree, or the score clears a value threshold: Sonnet/Opus pulls thread context, firm website, public records | Claude web search/fetch, HTML stripped | cents per item |
| 5. Cluster | Group extracted pains across sources to measure repetition and pick a vertical | BERTopic | ~$0 |
| 6. Human gate | You review a ranked queue showing evidence quotes and URLs; nothing is sent automatically | Simple internal page | your time |
| 7. Outcomes | Log reply, meeting and win against a holdout group; relabel weekly; retrain a small classifier once ~600 labels exist | SQLite/Postgres | ~$0 |

The first steps, in order:

1. **Pick one vertical where documents hurt and you already hold context.** Property and development (certifiers, town planners, builders handling DA documentation) is the obvious one, because your NSW planning pipeline is a proprietary signal source nobody else has.
2. **Hand-label 200–300 items** from legitimate sources as buyer or not, with reasons. This gives you a gold set and teaches you what the signal actually looks like.
3. **Build stages 2–3 and 6 in a weekend with Claude Code, and measure precision@20 on the gold set** before adding agents.
4. **Contact the top ten firms a week** through their published business channels with a specific, demonstrable fix, or reply publicly where a need was posted. Keep a random holdout.
5. **Build a demo of one solved workflow** (for example, extracting conditions of consent into a checklist) to anchor every conversation.

Only after a few paid engagements should you consider packaging the output as a "vertical lead feed" for others.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing from "find people complaining online" to "infer document pain from observable business situations". The tools that sell "AI intent" are mostly selling precision on needs people have already stated. The academic evidence says latent inference works only with labelled data and human review. And the richest discussion platforms have legally priced themselves out of reach for a solo operator. The defensible edge lies in proprietary or public-record signals, a small labelled set, and honest measurement against a holdout, not in a better Reddit scraper.

On the product question, the gap for a horizontal SaaS is mostly illusory. The top end is a funded oligopoly. The bottom end runs on thin margins, earning $272 all-time to about $3.3k MRR ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/whenasked)), and depends on platforms that can revoke access, as GummySearch learned. The real gap is a vertical, Australia-specific signal feed built on data others can't easily get. You'd earn the right to sell it by first proving it fills your own pipeline. Re-check before relying on them: Reddit's official policy text, OAIC guidance on the small-business exemption, and SEEK's terms. All three were unreachable or unverified in this research.
