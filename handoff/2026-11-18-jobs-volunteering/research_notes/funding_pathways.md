# Funding, Grants, Procurement and Other Paid Pathways for a Solo NSW Planning-Compliance / Housing-Data Developer (as of 18 November 2026)

> Method note: These notes come from web searches run on 2026-11-18. Direct page fetches of `nsw.gov.au`, `business.gov.au` and `info.buy.nsw.gov.au` were **blocked by this session's network allowlist**, so the official program pages could not be read in full. Facts below rest on search-result snippets of those official pages plus secondary sources. Everything marked "verify" should be confirmed on the official page before anyone relies on it. No program or amount was invented. Where something could not be found, it is listed under Gaps.

## 1. Grants for civic tech, open data, housing or AI (NSW, federal, philanthropic, international)

### Takeaway
As of mid-November 2026, **almost every relevant grant is closed or between rounds**. The NSW startup grants (MVP Ventures, Boosting Business Innovation / TechVouchers) have no open round. NLnet's NGI Zero Commons Fund has ended. The R&D Tax Incentive excludes sole traders. The most accessible near-term options are **in-kind** support (API credits), **federal Industry Growth Program advisory** (an adviser report must come before any grant, and sole-trader eligibility is unconfirmed), and **NLnet's regular call, which was due to reopen after the European summer of 2026**. Most commercialisation grants assume a company structure, so incorporating a Pty Ltd may be a precondition for several of them.

### Cited Findings

**NSW Government**
- **MVP Ventures (Investment NSW): closed, no 2026–27 round announced.** Round 3 of 2025–26 opened 9 March 2026 and closed 5 April 2026. Some sources say 10 April 2026. Grants are about $20,000 to $75,000. Late-October 2026 trackers report no open round. One noted on 28 Oct 2026 that the NSW page still shows Round 3 as "Current round". — [NSW: MVP Ventures 2025-26 Round 3](https://www.nsw.gov.au/grants-and-funding/mvp-ventures-2025-2026-round-3); [NSW: MVP Ventures Program](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/innovation/grants-and-programs/mvp-ventures-program); [Grantsights: Small Business Grants NSW 2026](https://grantsights.com/blog/small-business-grants-nsw); [Startup Daily: NSW revamps MVP grants with tighter rules and competitive selections](https://www.startupdaily.net/topic/funding/nsw-revamps-the-mvp-grants-program-with-tighter-rules-and-competitive-selections/)
- A search summary says a NSW program in these results (the Round 3 page) **excludes "an individual/sole trader"**. The full guidelines could not be fetched to confirm the wording, so verify. — [NSW: MVP Ventures 2025-26 Round 3](https://www.nsw.gov.au/grants-and-funding/mvp-ventures-2025-2026-round-3)
- **Boosting Business Innovation Program (BBIP), including TechVouchers: closed to new applications.** It funds partnerships between SMEs and Publicly Funded Research Organisations. TechVouchers are matched funding to work with a BBIP research partner. Total funding is $11M over four years from FY2023-24. NSW tells interested SMEs to contact a Delivery Partner (universities, CSIRO) directly. The recipients page lists approvals dated June and September 2026. One university's eligibility guidance asks for a NSW ABN and fewer than 200 staff. — [NSW: BBIP](https://www.nsw.gov.au/grants-and-funding/boosting-business-innovation-program); [business.gov.au: TechVouchers NSW](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/TechVouchers-NSW); [BBIP guidelines PDF](https://www.investment.nsw.gov.au/assets/Boosting-Business-Innovation-Program-incl.-TVs-guidelines-1.pdf); [UOW TechVouchers](https://uow.edu.au/industry/techvouchers); [NSW: TechVouchers recipients](https://www.nsw.gov.au/grants-and-funding/techvouchers/recipients)
- **Digital Restart Fund (DRF): not a grant for individuals.** It funds digital projects inside NSW Government. It has been redirected toward "innovation support" and toward the state's biggest challenges, explicitly including **housing supply**, frontline services and climate. Most past investment went to state agencies. Contact: digital.restart.fund@customerservice.nsw.gov.au. — [Digital NSW: DRF](https://digital.nsw.gov.au/funding/digital-restart-fund); [iTnews: NSW gov sets "new direction" for its $100m DRF](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/nsw-gov-sets-new-direction-for-its-100m-digital-restart-fund-602621); [RDA Riverina: funding digital solutions to target housing supply](https://rdariverina.org.au/news/2023/11/27/fresh-approach-to-funding-digital-solutions-to-target-housing-supply-and-frontline-services); [DRF 2025 project payments PDF](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/DRF-2025-ProjectPayments_0.pdf)

**Federal**
- **Industry Growth Program (IGP), Early-Stage Commercialisation:** grants of $50,000 to $250,000 for feasibility, proof-of-concept and prototyping. Getting an **IGP Advisory Service report is a mandatory step before applying**. The program has $287M committed over 2023-24 to 2026-27, so it runs out at the end of FY2026-27. The guidelines are dated May 2024. Sources disagree on the technology readiness range (TRL3–6 vs TRL4–9). **Sole-trader eligibility is not confirmed**; the program is framed around "SMEs". Contact: IGPGrants@industry.gov.au. — [business.gov.au: IGP](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/industry-growth-program); [IGP ESC guidelines PDF](https://business.gov.au/-/media/grants-and-programs/industry-growth-program/esc/industry-growth-program-early-stage-commercialisation-grant-opportunity-guidelines-pdf); [industry.gov.au: IGP launches advisory service](https://www.industry.gov.au/news/industry-growth-program-launches-advisory-service); [PKF: IGP for SMEs](https://www.pkf.com.au/insights/industry-growth-program-grant-opportunities-for-smes/)
- **R&D Tax Incentive: sole traders are not eligible.** It is available only to companies. These are secondary sources, so verify with the ATO or business.gov.au. — [Xero AU: R&D Tax Incentive guide](https://www.xero.com/au/guides/r-and-d-tax-incentive/); [Kreston: Australia R&D relief](https://www.kreston.com/r-and-d-relief/australia-rd-tax-relief-regime/)

**International open-source funds open to Australians**
- **NLnet: NGI Zero Commons Fund has ended.** Its 13th and final call closed 1 June 2026. NLnet then paused open submissions during a transition. The only open themes were NGI Taler (privacy-preserving payments) and NGI Fediversity (hosting stack), with a deadline of 1 August 2026, now past. NLnet said its regular call would **reopen "after the summer of 2026"**. Whether it has reopened by November 2026 was not confirmed. NLnet funds people worldwide. — [NLnet Commons Fund](https://www.nlnet.nl/commonsfund); [NLnet: Apply before August 1st 2026](https://nlnet.nl/news/2026/20260601-call.html); [NLnet: Taler/Fediversity deadline Aug 1 2026](https://nlnet.nl/news/2026/20260612-NGIZero-stocktaking.html)
- **Sovereign Tech Agency (Germany):** the **Sovereign Tech Fund** invests in "open digital base technologies worldwide" and works with people, companies and communities "everywhere". It is aimed at foundational infrastructure, so an applied NSW planning tool is a weak fit. Projects already publicly funded for the same work are ineligible. The **Sovereign Tech Fellowship** 2026 cohort closed on 6 April 2026, and its employment option is limited to people in Germany. — [Sovereign Tech Fund: applications](https://sovereign.tech/programs/fund); [Sovereign Tech FAQ](https://sovereign.tech/faq); [Fellowship FAQ](https://sovereign.tech/programs/fellowship/faq); [Phoronix: Fellowship 2026](https://phoronix.com/news/sovereign-tech-fellowship-2026)
- **Anthropic AI for Science Program (in-kind):** free API credits for researchers in academia and nonprofits working on high-priority **scientific** research, with a focus on biology and life sciences. It has offered up to $20,000 in credits over 6 months. An August 2026 expansion reportedly allows up to $50,000 per project. Selections are made monthly, on the first Monday. Credits are API-only, not Claude.ai. This is a poor fit for planning compliance unless the work is framed as research with a university or nonprofit partner. — [Anthropic: AI for Science program](https://www.anthropic.com/news/ai-for-science-program); [Claude help center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11199177-anthropics-ai-for-science-program); [Program rules](https://www.anthropic.com/ai-for-science-program-rules); [Kindora listing](https://www.kindora.co/en/in-kind/anthropic-ai-for-science-program-anthropic-pbc)

**Philanthropic / fellowship-style**

### Inferences
- Several programs (R&D Tax Incentive, likely MVP Ventures, possibly IGP) favour companies, so **registering a Pty Ltd** is probably a prerequisite for most commercialisation money. A sole trader can still sell services and sell to government.
- The DRF's housing-supply focus suggests the realistic route to DRF money is **indirect**: becoming a subcontractor or supplier to a NSW agency (DPHI, the Housing Delivery Authority area) that holds DRF funding, not applying yourself.
- The most actionable grant step right now is the **IGP Advisory Service**. It is free and mandatory before applying, and the program ends in FY2026-27, so the window is narrowing.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch official guidelines (network block) to confirm MVP Ventures' and IGP's exact entity-type eligibility.
- Not researched or not found: CSIRO Kick-Start current status; Myer Foundation, Lord Mayor's Charitable Foundation and Australian Communities Foundation open rounds; Housing Australia or AHURI research funding open to individuals; Mozilla, GitHub Secure Open Source Fund, OpenAI and Google.org programs. Status for these is **unknown**, not "none".
- Whether NLnet's regular call actually reopened in autumn 2026.

## 2. Government procurement for small suppliers (NSW, councils, federal)

### Takeaway
Procurement is the **most concrete paid pathway** for a sole trader. NSW agencies may buy ICT **directly from an SME up to $250,000 (ex GST)**, even where a whole-of-government contract exists. Suppliers register through buy.nsw and the ICT Services Scheme. Federally, sellers register on **BuyICT** to join the Digital Marketplace Panel 2, which reopens periodically to new sellers.

### Cited Findings
- **NSW SME direct purchase:** agencies may buy ICT direct from an SME **up to $250,000** regardless of whole-of-government contracts. The limit excludes GST and has exemptions, such as telecommunications purchasing arrangements. ICT consultancy must still follow the ICT Consultancy Framework. The ICT Purchasing Framework is mandatory for all ICT procurement. — [buy.nsw: ICT/Digital SME procurement commitments](https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/resources/ictdigital-sovereign-procurement-commitments); [buy.nsw: ICT category](https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/categories/ict)
- The SME and Regional Procurement Policy **raised the direct-procurement threshold for SMEs from $150,000 to $250,000**. There is a target of **30% of addressable ICT spend going directly to SMEs**. The commitments document found is dated December 2022, so verify the current version. — [buy.nsw: SME and Regional Procurement Policy](https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/policy-library/policies/sme-and-regional-procurement-policy); [NSW: major changes to small business procurement](https://www.nsw.gov.au/media-releases/major-changes-to-small-business-procurement); [buy.nsw: supporting SMEs](https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/buyer-guidance/source/select-suppliers/supporting-smes)
- **ICT Services Scheme (PBD-2025-01):** NSW's approved procurement arrangement for ICT services. For ICT consultancy up to $250,000, one written quote is acceptable from suppliers who have accepted the ICT Consulting Commercial Framework. — [ARP NSW: PBD-2025-01 ICT Services Scheme](https://arp.nsw.gov.au/pbd-2025-01-approved-procurement-arrangements-for-the-ict-services-scheme); [buy.nsw: low-value contracts](https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/buyer-guidance/source/select-suppliers/low-value-contracts)
- **Federal BuyICT / Digital Marketplace Panel 2 (DMP2):** sellers register and apply through BuyICT.gov.au, with tender details on AusTender. DTA describes DMP2 as a flexible panel that lets **new suppliers apply through regular requests**. Approved sellers join the Seller Catalogue. — [DTA: DMP2 open for business (Oct 2024)](https://www.dta.gov.au/media-release/digital-marketplace-panel-2-open-business); [DTA: DMP2 now open for applications](https://www.dta.gov.au/blogs/digital-marketplace-panel-2-now-open-applications); [TechPartner: DMP2 invites new sellers](https://www.techpartner.news/news/digital-marketplace-panel-2-invites-new-sellers-621451); [AusTender ATM](https://www.tenders.gov.au/Atm/Show/440e53c2-486f-4c78-bba3-dd54d471e5e0)

### Inferences
- A sole trader with an ABN who is listed on the NSW ICT Services Scheme can be engaged directly by a NSW agency for work under $250k. That is large enough for a pilot or data product, such as DCP control extraction or a compliance-checking dashboard, without a tender.
- Councils make most residential DA decisions (see §3), so **councils are a natural buyer**. Council procurement (Local Government Procurement, Local Buy) was not verified here.

### Gaps
- Exact current steps and requirements for a sole trader joining the NSW ICT Services Scheme (insurance levels and so on). The page fetch was blocked.
- Local Government Procurement (LGP) and Local Buy council panels: thresholds and how to get listed were not researched. A council magazine article mentions an "LGP fund supporting tech innovation" — [Council Magazine](https://councilmagazine.com.au/lgp-fund-supporting-tech-innovation/) — but its details were not verified.
- NSW "Test & Buy" or innovation-challenge programs: not found or verified.

## 3. Housing-supply policy context that creates demand (NSW 2025–2026)

### Takeaway
NSW has a hard target of **377,000 new homes by mid-2029** under the National Housing Accord (1.2M nationally). It publishes a **council performance league table** on DA speed and has layered on new planning controls (low and mid-rise housing, TOD). Councils are under public pressure to assess faster under new, complex rules. That creates demand from councils, DPHI and developers for tools that work out which controls apply to a site.

### Cited Findings
- NSW's Accord share is **377,000 well-located homes over five years to mid-2029**, with targets split across 43 LGAs. — [NSW Planning: housing targets FAQ](https://planning.nsw.gov.au/the-planning-system/housing/housing-targets/frequently-asked-questions); [Urbis: Getting to 1.2 million](https://urbis.com.au/perspectives/getting-to-12-million-nsw-sets-agenda-to-meet-its-target)
- A **council league table** launched in July 2024 under the Faster Assessments program. It ranks councils on DA assessment, planning proposals and strategic planning, alongside a Statement of Expectations Order. — [NSW Planning: council performance to be ranked](https://planning.nsw.gov.au/news/council-performance-be-ranked-meet-housing-targets); [Inside Local Government](https://insidelocalgovernment.com.au/planning-league-table-kicks-off-for-nsw-councils); [Government News](https://www.governmentnews.com.au/councils-to-be-ranked-on-housing-league-table/)
- Councils process **about 85% of residential DAs** (Property Council). — [Property Council: Council DA performance off to a strong start](https://www.propertycouncil.com.au/media-releases/council-da-performance-off-to-a-strong-start-property-council)
- Approvals lag: **28,984 dwellings were approved in the Accord's first nine months** (about 3,220 a month), well short of the target rate. — [Build Australia](https://www.buildaustralia.com.au/news_article/nsw-housing-approvals-lag-behind-2029-targets/)
- The March 2026 National Housing Supply and Affordability Council quarterly report projects NSW could meet its share by Q3 2029, but modelling suggests it could slip to June 2031, and approvals are a risk. — [The Urban Developer](https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/national-housing-accord-2025-26-q1-report)
- The **Low and Mid-Rise Housing Policy** took effect on 28 February 2025. It permits terraces, townhouses and 3–6 storey apartments near transport and centres. Later amendments extended it, for example by changing aircraft-noise thresholds. The **TOD program** is more permissive and prevails where the two overlap. Councils such as Woollahra and Ku-ring-gai object. — [Ku-ring-gai: changes to NSW housing policy](https://www.krg.nsw.gov.au/Planning-and-development/Changes-to-NSW-Government-housing-policy); [Paul Scully MP: L&MR to unlock 112,000 homes](https://paulscullymp.com.au/news/media-releases/low-and-mid-rise-policy-to-unlock-112-000-homes-in-five-years/); [Paul Scully MP: 37 TOD hubs in force](https://paulscullymp.com.au/news/media-releases/37-hubs-for-transport-homes-and-jobs-now-in-full-force/); [Woollahra Your Say](https://yoursay.woollahra.nsw.gov.au/housing)
- The NSW Housing Taskforce and Housing Delivery Authority exist to fast-track large projects. — [NSW Planning: Housing Taskforce](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/policy-and-legislation/housing/housing-delivery-authority/nsw-housing-taskforce)

### Inferences
- Buyers: (a) **councils**, ranked publicly on DA speed and having to apply overlapping state controls on top of their DCPs; (b) **DPHI**, which is monitoring delivery and has DRF funding aimed at housing supply; (c) **developers and consultants** doing site feasibility under the new low and mid-rise and TOD rules.
- Existing commercial players (Archistar, Landchecker) show developers and councils already pay for site-intelligence tools (§5).

### Gaps
- Current (Q3/Q4 2026) league-table figures and any 2026 planning reforms (for example a planning bill or pattern-book complying-development changes) were not confirmed from official sources.

## 5. Case studies: volunteer or open civic tech to funded work (Australia)

### Takeaway
The two clearest Australian examples point in opposite directions. **OpenAustralia Foundation** (PlanningAlerts) stayed a donation- and grant-funded charity with small revenue and a tiny individual-donor base, plus commercial subscriptions for high-volume users. **Archistar** took the venture-backed proptech route and has raised roughly A$12–21M since 2018. No verified case was found of a solo volunteer project turning into council contracts.

### Cited Findings
- **OpenAustralia Foundation / PlanningAlerts:** it describes itself as funded by donations from users and as a registered charity. Its about page says donations are not currently tax-deductible, but a 2023 ATO-related letter refers to tax-deductible donations, so the sources conflict. It relied on "volunteers, donations and a few grants" and received start-up funding from Google (Wikipedia). It has said tens of thousands of users produce very little donation income. **High-volume commercial users pay a subscription** for PlanningAlerts. An aggregator (unverified) lists revenue of about $315K in 2023. — [OAF: About](https://www.oaf.org.au/about/); [OAF ATO finalisation letter 2023 (PDF)](https://www.oaf.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/20231116_Finalisation-Letter_OpenAustralia-Foundation-Limited_redacted-1.pdf); [Wikipedia: OpenAustralia Foundation](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/OpenAustralia_Foundation); [OAF: Support us](https://www.oaf.org.au/donate/alternatives/); [CivicGraph (aggregator)](https://civicgraph.app/entities/AU-ABN-24138089942)
- **Archistar:** founded in 2018 by architect Dr Benjamin Coorey (with his brother Rob), with about $1.5M seed. Series A of about $6M was led by AirTree in 2020; one source says $4M. A further $6M came in April 2021 (Skip Capital, AirTree), and $11M in June 2022 (NAB Ventures, Skip, Skyfield, AirTree). CB Insights lists $3.3M in June 2024. Database totals vary: $12M (ZoomInfo), $12.88M (Caplight), $20.84M over six rounds (CB Insights). Archistar sells to councils as well as developers. — [Archistar blog: raises $6m](https://archistar.ai/blog/archistar-raises-6m-after-record-year-of-growth/); [Business News Australia](https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/amp-html/articles/property-and-planning-design-intelligence-venture-archistar-raises--6m-for-global-ambitions.html); [AECCafe: $6m Series A led by AirTree](https://aeccafe.com/nbc/articles/1/1752207/Bluechip-backed-generative-design-platform-Archistar-secures-$6m-Series-investment-led-by-AirTree-Ventures); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/archistar/financials); [Juwai 2018](https://list.juwai.com/press/2018/12/Archistar-mou-partners)
- Archistar was not open source or volunteer-origin. It is included as a commercial comparator, not as a volunteer-to-paid case.

### Inferences
- The OAF model suggests a hybrid revenue pattern: free public access plus paid commercial API or data subscriptions. That transfers directly to a planning-controls dataset.
- Archistar's council customers suggest councils will pay for planning tech. Statements about its council contracts were not separately verified here.

### Gaps
- Landchecker's origins and funding were not researched.
- No verified Australian case of an individual open-source civic tech maintainer moving into paid government contracts was found within budget. This is a **gap, not evidence that none exist**.
