# Sell the rigour, publish the guardrails first

**Bottom line:** Lead with paid work and treat open source and volunteering as evidence for it, not as goals in their own right. The market for generalist developers in late 2026 is soft. Australian job ads were still **down 3.5% year on year in September 2026** ([SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/australian-job-ads-edge-up-06-in-september)), and WiseTech, Atlassian and Canva all cut engineers this year. But the market prices this profile very differently depending on its label. As an "AI engineer" it is worth about **A$140–170k base, or A$900–1,100 a day on contract**. As a "GIS analyst" it is worth about A$64–96k (self-reported Payscale data, weak). So position yourself as an applied-AI / data engineer who specialises in geospatial planning data. Your strongest asset is not the 55k provisions. It is the proof that you can run LLM extraction under strict QA, with integrity checks for fabricated values, gate scripts, test ratchets and a defect tracker. Few candidates can show that. Over the next two weeks, turn the 76-contact outreach into specific introductions. Publish one small, low-risk open-source repo that shows the guardrail tooling. Apply through the two channels with real NSW demand: DPHI's ePlanning roles and contract agencies on the Contingent Workforce Scheme. Register on buy.nsw, because NSW agencies can buy ICT directly from an SME for **up to A$250,000** without a tender. Grants are close to useless right now: almost every relevant round is closed, and most assume a company. Do not open-source the existing repo. Its LICENSE names another author, it tracks 91 MB of database dumps, and its history has never been scanned for secrets. Release clean, purpose-built repos instead. One caveat on evidence: most official sites (iworkfor, SEEK, buy.nsw, nsw.gov.au, business.gov.au, Planning Portal, GovHack, OAF) were blocked during research. Many facts below come from search snippets, and the verification table at the end lists what to confirm.

## Pay follows the job title, and NSW Government is the clearest buyer

### The market is soft overall but pays a premium for AI work

The headline figures are weak. SEEK ads rose in August 2026 for the first time in over a year ([SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/australian-job-ads-rise-in-august-for-the-first-time-in-over-a-year)). July had the highest number of applications per ad recorded ([SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/job-ads-in-australia-fall-in-july-applications-per-ad-reach-high)). ACS reports that Australia's ICT industry **shrank for the first time** in 2026 and that only six tech occupations are still in shortage ([ACS Information Age](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2026/only-six-tech-jobs-now-face-worker-shortages-in-australia.html); snippet only, list not retrieved). The big local software employers are shedding engineers. WiseTech announced about 2,000 cuts tied to AI coding ([DCN](https://www.thedcn.com.au/news/wisetech-to-slash-2000-jobs-amid-ai-disruption)). Canva cut or relocated **about 1,271 roles** on 4 November 2026 ([HCA Mag](https://www.hcamag.com/ca/news/general/canva-job-cuts-affect-1271-roles-in-first-redundancy-round/596053)). That puts a supply of laid-off Sydney engineers on the market just as you are looking. TechnologyOne, which sells ERP and planning software to councils, is the counter-example: it has grown to 1,600+ staff and is avoiding AI layoffs ([Reuters via TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026:newsml_L3N46Y02T:0-australia-s-technologyone-resists-wave-of-ai-job-cuts-to-boost-productivity/)).

Pay depends on how you are labelled. Galileo Search's July 2026 data, based on advertised rates and weighted to Sydney, puts AI engineer median base at **A$140k mid and A$170k senior**, with contract medians of **A$923/day mid and A$1,120/day senior** ([Galileo](https://galileosearch.com.au/ai-engineer-salary-australia)). This is a recruiter source, but it lines up with Robert Walters' NSW figures of A$800–1,150/day as Galileo quotes them. Self-reported Payscale data puts a Sydney GIS analyst at **A$64–96k** ([Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/research/AU/Job=Geographic_Information_Systems_(GIS)_Analyst/Salary/01cd4d9b/Sydney)). The same skills earn roughly 60% more under the AI label. Write your CV, LinkedIn headline and outreach as "applied AI / data engineer: LLM extraction with evaluation and QA, PostGIS, NSW planning data". Do not lead with "GIS" or "planning compliance". Global freelance marketplaces are a trap at this level. Typical worldwide Upwork AI-automation budgets are US$30–60/hr, while one Australia-based contract was posted at **A$80–130/hr** ([Upwork AU listing](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Australian-Power-Platform-Contractor-Power-Automate-SharePoint-Azure-Document-Intelligence_~022067104751174188207/)). Hiring through Australian agencies or directly is worth three to five times more per hour.

### DPHI's ePlanning program is the most direct domain match

The NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure is the best-evidenced live employer for this exact domain. Aggregators showed **nine open DPHI roles** at search time. They included a Principal PEGA Application Developer on Planning Portal modernisation (temporary, up to 12 months, posted around 17 Nov), a Principal Project Manager (Planning Portal) **closing 22 November 2026**, and a Senior Application Developer (Spatial) that closed in September at **A$133,348–146,945 plus super** ([freehire DPHI](https://freehire.me/companies/department-of-planning-housing-and-infrastructure-nsw); [Senior Spatial role](https://freehire.me/jobs/senior-application-developer-department-of-planning-housing-and-infrastructure-nsw-emtj2syl)). This comes from a single aggregator, so confirm every listing on iworkfor before applying. DPHI's stack centres on PEGA and Oracle, so the PEGA role suits you only if you have PEGA experience. Set an iworkfor alert for "spatial", "data" and "ePlanning" roles, which have offices in Parramatta, Orange and Newcastle. Your knowledge of the Portal's APIs, EPI layers and CDC rules overlaps directly with what that team runs.

For contract work, individuals usually reach NSW agencies through a recruiter that is prequalified on the mandatory **Contingent Workforce Scheme (SCM0007)**, which runs to Feb 2028 ([buy.nsw CWS](https://info.buy.nsw.gov.au/schemes/contingent-workforce-scheme)). The Department of Education requires extra approval for contractor rates above **A$1,110/day** ([NSW DoE policy](https://education.nsw.gov.au/policy-library/policies/pd-2024-0484-03)). That points to a practical ceiling of about A$1,100/day for government developer contracts. No current CWS supplier list was found, so ask each recruiter directly whether they are on it.

### Private and research employers worth a direct approach

Archistar is the most domain-aligned private company. It describes itself as AI-powered planning and permitting software, and it had five open roles in October 2026, mostly in sales and product management ([startup.jobs](https://startup.jobs/company/archistar)). It has raised roughly **A$12–21M**, depending on the database ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/archistar/financials)), and sells to councils. UNSW City Futures is the stronger target. It leads the A$6.5M Urban Copilot CRC-P, a generative-AI assistant for planners, with Archistar, PEXA and its own spinout **MapAI** ([UNSW City Futures](https://www.unsw.edu.au/research/city-futures/our-research/urban-analytics)). It also runs the A$1M Housing Analytics Lab, which plans a dashboard of planning approvals ([AURIN](https://aurin.org.au/1-million-in-funding-for-new-housing-analytics-lab/)). Those two projects line up with your LLM extraction and DA-outcome analytics more closely than anything else the research found. Landchecker, Giraffe, Cotality, PEXA, InfoTrack, the big engineering consultancies and council digital teams were **not checked** for hiring, so no claim is made either way.

### What the evidence says gets people hired, including on age

Hard Australian evidence is thin. In a BairesDev survey, senior developers weighted practical project experience (70%) above internships ([IT Brief](https://itbrief.com.au/story/ai-tools-widen-gap-in-junior-software-engineers-skills)). In a 4,040-developer global survey, 37% named GitHub as the best signal of real skill, against 14% for LinkedIn ([daily.dev](https://daily.dev/blog/where-developers-look-for-jobs-2026/)). The claim that the best roles "never reach job boards" comes from a vendor blog with no data. If you are mid-career or older, the bias data matters. In AHRI/AHRC's survey of 148 HR professionals, **24%** now class people in their early 50s as "older", and **only 1%** think older workers have better tech skills ([AHRI](https://www.ahri.com.au/articles/why-our-definition-of-an-older-worker-is-shifting)). University of Melbourne research warns that AI screening tools can filter out applicants over 45 ([Pursuit](https://pursuit.unimelb.edu.au/articles/over-45-and-looking-for-a-job-ai-thinks-you-might-be-too-old)). A public, inspectable repo with test counts and eval reports answers the "tech skills" prejudice directly. Warm introductions bypass the automated screen. Both point to the same plan. On the 76 messages: no Australian data on cold-outreach or LinkedIn response rates was found, so judge the result by introductions obtained, not replies. Following up with existing professional contacts carries little legal risk. Do not scale this into scraped lists: the Spam Act requires consent for commercial email, and the sender bears the onus of proof ([Allens](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/explore/2025/food-and-beverage-law-bulletin-2025/emerging-regulator-views-and-enforcement-risks-in-direct-marketing-and-spam/)).

## Open-source the guardrails and state data, not the repo

### The existing repo cannot be published as-is

Measured on 18 November 2026 in a shallow clone, the repo has **6,868 tracked files**, `.git` is **446 MB**, and **25 tracked files totalling 91 MB sit under `backups/`** (`git ls-files backups | wc -l`). Those files are Postgres and SQLite dumps of regulatory tables, and one dump contains an email address. An earlier note gave 106 backup files. That figure cannot be reproduced, and the likely source is the 251 tracked paths that contain "backup" anywhere in the path. The LICENSE reads **"MIT License, Copyright (c) 2025 Cole Medin"**. That is probably inherited from his public context-engineering template, which is an unverified inference. As written, it tells anyone who reads it that someone else licensed your entire project, code and data, under MIT. Strategy and sales documents (`plans/strategic/`, `council-sales-playbook.md`, `.claude/gtm-strategy-2026`) and the internal product names that your own rules treat as IP would also become public. The visible history alone adds **320 data files** (SQL, CSV and similar), and the full history has never been scanned for secrets. Two code defects would also undercut a "rigour" showcase. `autoschema_kg_query.py` silently returns empty results when CSVs are missing. `clause_citation_inline.py` contains a hardcoded `fake_to_real_mapping`. GitHub's own guidance is that rewriting history does not undo exposure, cached PR views need GitHub Support to remove, and every exposed secret must be rotated ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/keeping-your-account-and-data-secure/removing-sensitive-data-from-a-repository)). The answer is to keep the private repo as the source of truth and publish **new repos from clean trees**, not to scrub 446 MB of history.

Fix the LICENSE in the private repo now. Put your own name or entity on it. If any template files remain, keep the original MIT text for those files in a `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES` file. If the repo was ever public, anyone who received it may hold an MIT licence to that version **[LAWYER if it was ever public]**.

The licence choices follow from the goals. **Use Apache-2.0 for libraries.** It is OSI-approved, so "open source" reads cleanly on a CV, and its express patent grant reassures larger employers. Use AGPL-3.0 only if you later publish the hosted server and want modified hosted copies kept open. Note that Open Core Ventures calls AGPL "a non-starter for most companies" ([OCV](https://opencoreventures.com/blog/agpl-license-is-a-non-starter-for-most-companies)). FSL converts to Apache after two years, but it is **not OSI-approved** ([fsl.software](https://fsl.software/)), which weakens the employment signal. Keep every copyright yourself until you decide. Dual licensing needs sole ownership, so add a CLA or DCO-plus-assignment before you accept outside pull requests. The licence sources here are mostly vendor and opinion pieces. No authoritative 2025–26 legal survey was found.

### Why state data is safe and council DCPs are not

The inbound licences decide this. NSW legislation is published under **CC BY 4.0**, with set attribution wording for altered and unaltered content ([NSW Legislation copyright](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/copyright); snippet only). Planning Portal EPI datasets carry CC BY 4.0, attributed to DPHI ([Planning Portal open data](https://www.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/opendata/dataset/environmental-planning-instrument-permitted-uses)). Spatial Services data is CC BY 4.0 ([spatial.nsw.gov.au](https://www.spatial.nsw.gov.au/copyright)). Two wrinkles need checking. The PCC and CDC data API listings say "Creative Commons Attribution" with no version. Some data.gov.au mirrors still list **CC BY 3.0 AU** ([data.gov.au](https://www.data.gov.au/data/dataset/nsw-environmental-planning-instrument-local-provisions/resource/127e3437-be5e-4311-a84d-ab7118480850)). Council DCPs are a patchwork. The Blacktown Growth Centre DCP is CC BY 4.0. The Catherine Hill Bay DCP allows only "personal, in-house or non-commercial use" ([Lake Macquarie PDF](https://lakemac.com.au/files/assets/public/v/1/hptrim/land-use-and-planning-planning-development-control-plans-dcp-development-control-plan-dcp-no-1-revision-10-catherine-hill-bay-development-control-plan/catherine-hill-bay-rose-group-land-development-control-plan/catherine-hill-bay-south-development-control-plan-july-2012.pdf)). Port Macquarie-Hastings uses similar terms ([PMHC](https://www.pmhc.nsw.gov.au/System-Pages/Copyright-Disclaimer)). Australia has no database right. *Telstra v Phone Directories* applied *IceTV* and held that a highly automated compilation lacked human authorship, displacing "sweat of the brow" ([Federal Court](https://www.judgments.fedcourt.gov.au/judgments/Judgments/fca/full/2010/2010fcafc0149)). That cuts two ways. Extracted numeric controls are on stronger ground than verbatim clause text. But your own LLM-extracted output may attract little copyright either, so a restrictive data licence protects little and mainly deters the employers and buyers you want. This reasoning is an inference, not a safe harbour **[LAWYER]**. The 2026 Copyright Amendment Act added an orphan-works scheme and only clarified Crown copyright duration ([Bills Digest](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/bd/bd2526/26bd33)). It does not change the position of council DCPs. Whether councils count as "the Crown" was not verified **[LAWYER]**. For models to follow, use the Open Australian Legal Corpus's per-source licence table ([HF dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/isaacus/open-australian-legal-corpus)) and NYC's disclaimer that zoning data is "not intended for determining zoning at the individual tax lot level" ([NYC metadata](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/data-maps/open-data/nyzd_metadata.pdf?r=1219)). The NYC wording fits your own liability-language rules. Exclude anything carrying personal information, such as objector contact details, which NCAT treated as personal information in *DRP v Orange City Council* ([IPC case note](https://www.ipc.nsw.gov.au/node/1700)).

As for what the release does for you: no rigorous 2025–26 study shows that GitHub repos get people hired. The widely quoted "70% of employers view OSS favourably" figure has no primary source, so do not repeat it. Qualitative work with US hiring managers finds they read open-source work as evidence of initiative and judgement ([arXiv 2510.25180](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25180v1)). Treat the repo as something to send with an introduction, not a magnet that brings employers to you.

## Volunteer where it leads to paid work

Most civic-tech volunteering in Australia does not convert into income. The best-documented path is unpaid volunteer to OAF board member, in the case of Henare Degan ([OAF about](https://www.oaf.org.au/about/)). No GovHack project was found that became a funded company or government contract. So pick volunteer work for **who will see it**, not for the cause alone.

**UNSW City Futures is the top target, even unpaid.** A cleaned DCP-controls or DA-outcome dataset offered to the Housing Analytics Lab, or an evaluation harness offered to the Urban Copilot team, puts your work in front of the people building the most similar systems in NSW. They are also tied commercially to Archistar, PEXA and MapAI. Note that AURIN's process for independent contributors was not confirmed.

**OpenAustralia Foundation is the best public venue, but it will not pay.** PlanningAlerts still runs mostly per-council NSW scrapers on morph.io. The NSW major-projects scraper's last run had **failed with a timeout** ([morph.io](https://morph.io/planningalerts-scrapers/nsw_planning_major_projects)). Meanwhile DPHI keeps a statewide, CC BY Online DA Data API covering portal-lodged DAs since 2019, with four release notes in 2026 ([data.gov.au](https://www.data.gov.au/data/dataset/nsw-online-da-data-api)). Fixing NSW coverage, possibly through that API, is high-leverage work you already know how to do. Ask the maintainers first, because they may already ingest it. PlanningAlerts appears not to serve outcomes or applicable controls, which is your niche. OAF is funded by donations, and its banner warns the "library will close" without supporters ([PlanningAlerts](https://www.planningalerts.org.au/alerts/signup)), so treat it as a reputation venue only. It collected **198,020 DAs in FY2025-26** ([OAF review](https://oaf.org.au/2026/07/17/a-year-of-putting-democracy-in-more-hands-our-2025-26-in-review/)), and that reach is the payoff.

**Use the community venues for networking.** Code for Sydney holds monthly hack days at Darling Square Library and has about 398 Meetup members ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/codeforsydney)). GovHack NSW includes a pre-event session to meet data owners ([GovHack NSW](https://govhack.org/locations/new-south-wales/)). That session is the useful part: it puts you in a room with data.nsw and DPHI staff. The 2026 dates could not be confirmed. GovHack normally runs in late August, so plan to mentor or volunteer as an organiser for 2027 rather than expecting a near-term event. A Code for Australia Fellowship is a paid placement inside government for up to a year ([CfA](https://cfa-website.vercel.app/fellowship/)), but no 2026 intake was found.

**Advocacy groups have uses for your data but no stated demand.** The Abundant Housing Network has called for standardised planning data formats across jurisdictions ([AHNA submission, 2024](https://infrastructure.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/nupc-abundant-housing-network-australia.pdf)). Sydney YIMBY, Shelter NSW and the Tenants' Union make submissions on planning reform. Analysis showing which lots sit in TOD or low- and mid-rise areas, against heritage and flood constraints, is an inferred fit, not a documented request. Offer it as research data, never as advice to legal centres.

## Procurement beats grants for a solo builder right now

### Grants are mostly closed or need a company

Almost every relevant grant is shut or between rounds as of 18 November 2026. NSW MVP Ventures (about A$20–75k) last closed in April 2026, no new round has been announced, and a snippet suggests it excludes sole traders ([NSW MVP Ventures](https://www.nsw.gov.au/grants-and-funding/mvp-ventures-2025-2026-round-3)). BBIP TechVouchers is closed to new applications ([NSW BBIP](https://www.nsw.gov.au/grants-and-funding/boosting-business-innovation-program)). The R&D Tax Incentive is **companies only**, according to secondary sources ([Xero AU](https://www.xero.com/au/guides/r-and-d-tax-incentive/)). NLnet's Commons Fund ended on 1 June 2026, and whether its regular call has reopened is unconfirmed ([NLnet](https://nlnet.nl/news/2026/20260601-call.html)). NLnet also favours applicants with an EU dimension. The Sovereign Tech Fund funds foundational infrastructure, not applied tools ([Sovereign Tech](https://sovereign.tech/programs/fund)). The one live option is the federal **Industry Growth Program**. It offers A$50–250k early-stage commercialisation grants, and a free IGP Advisory Service report is **mandatory** before applying ([business.gov.au IGP](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/industry-growth-program)). Its funding runs only to FY2026-27, and eligibility for sole traders is unconfirmed. Book the advisory session only if you decide to build a product company. Otherwise skip grants for now. The NSW Digital Restart Fund targets housing supply, but it funds agencies, not individuals ([Digital NSW](https://digital.nsw.gov.au/funding/digital-restart-fund)). The way to that money is as a supplier to an agency that holds DRF funding.

### Direct SME buying is the concrete paid pathway

NSW agencies may buy ICT **directly from an SME for up to A$250,000 (ex GST)**, even where a whole-of-government contract exists. The policy also sets a target of 30% of addressable ICT spend going to SMEs ([buy.nsw SME commitments](https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/resources/ictdigital-sovereign-procurement-commitments)). The commitments document found is dated December 2022, so verify the current version. Agencies must buy ICT from **ICT Services Scheme** suppliers. New suppliers can apply at any time until 28 Feb 2029, after first creating a buy.nsw account ([buy.nsw ICT Services Scheme](https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/schemes/ict-services-scheme)). For ICT consultancy under A$250k, a single written quote can be enough ([buy.nsw low-value contracts](https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/buyer-guidance/source/select-suppliers/low-value-contracts)). Federally, sellers register on BuyICT for the Digital Marketplace Panel 2, which reopens to new sellers periodically ([DTA](https://www.dta.gov.au/blogs/digital-marketplace-panel-2-now-open-applications)).

Policy pressure creates the demand. NSW must deliver **377,000 homes by mid-2029** ([NSW Planning](https://planning.nsw.gov.au/the-planning-system/housing/housing-targets/frequently-asked-questions)) and ranks councils on DA speed in a public league table ([NSW Planning](https://planning.nsw.gov.au/news/council-performance-be-ranked-meet-housing-targets)). Councils handle about **85% of residential DAs** ([Property Council](https://www.propertycouncil.com.au/media-releases/council-da-performance-off-to-a-strong-start-property-council)), and they now have to layer low- and mid-rise and TOD controls on top of their own DCPs. Approvals are lagging: 28,984 dwellings were approved in the Accord's first nine months ([Build Australia](https://www.buildaustralia.com.au/news_article/nsw-housing-approvals-lag-behind-2029-targets/)). The productisable offer is a fixed-scope pilot: digitise one council's DCP into structured, cited controls with a QA report, or deliver a DA-outcome analysis for one LGA, priced well under the A$250k direct-buy limit. Council panels (LGP, Local Buy) were not researched, so ask your first council contact how they buy. The earlier intent-discovery work still applies here. Use public records, such as DA lodgement streams that show which consultants and builders are active, to time and personalise outreach to firms through their published business channels. Do not build per-person lead lists. The OAF model of free public access plus paid high-volume subscriptions ([OAF support](https://www.oaf.org.au/donate/alternatives/)) is a fair template if the dataset gains users.

## What to verify before acting

Snippet-level evidence carries the most risk wherever a deadline, an eligibility rule or a licence term drives a decision. Evidence grades used here: **official (snippet)** means an official page seen only through a search summary. **Single-source** means one aggregator or report. **Vendor** means a source with a commercial interest.
